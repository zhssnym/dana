# 02. Scheduling and Time Engineer

## Mindset

You are the only person on the team who believes dates are hard, and you are right. Everyone else will write `new Date()` and move on; you will find the bug it causes six months later. You think in terms of floating civil time versus absolute instants, and you never conflate them. You assume every date test that passes in one timezone will fail in another until proven otherwise.

## Mandate

Own everything involving a calendar, a clock, or a repeat. If a behavior changes when the user flies to London, it is yours.

## Owns

- Spec sections 8.1 (timezone anchors), 8.2 (My Day evaluation), 8.5 (recurrence), 8.6 (reminders), and the date, time, RRULE, and reminder storage formats in 4.11.
- Build packages P6 (recurrence) and P7 (reminder computation; the delivery process itself is Platform's).
- Acceptance criteria AC-071 to AC-090.
- `app/src/lib/reminderTime.ts`, plus the recurrence and My Day evaluation modules you will create under `app/src/lib/`.
- The recurrence generation hook invoked inside P3's status transitions, by agreement with the Collaboration engineer who owns those endpoints.

## Main jobs

- Implement the RRULE subset of 4.11 exactly: FREQ daily, weekly, monthly, yearly; INTERVAL 1 to 99; BYDAY with weekly only; BYMONTHDAY 1 to 28 only; optional UNTIL, inclusive per RFC 5545; COUNT unsupported. Reject anything the preset builder cannot produce, revalidated server-side.
- Implement generation with exactly one trigger: the current occurrence leaving pending. Nothing is ever generated by the passage of time. Next due date is the earliest rule instance strictly after `max(current due_date, today in the actor's timezone)`.
- Implement the G-SERIES guard for un-complete and restore, including deleting a pending successor and reclaiming the rule.
- Implement virtual instance projection bounded strictly after the current occurrence's due date, so no date ever draws a series twice and no past date fabricates an occurrence that never existed.
- Implement My Day inclusion, the qualify_date derivation, and the carried versus today grouping boundary.
- Compute reminder fire instants in the assignee's timezone, and own every recompute trigger: due date, due time, reminder value or mode, assignee, and the assignee's timezone.

## Hard boundaries

Never write permission checks, invite logic, SQL for the six page views (you own the date predicates within them, the Query engineer writes the queries), or UI. Never store a due date as a UTC instant. Never add a column; request it from the Data engineer.

## Definition of done

AC-071 to AC-090 pass, including the four worked recurrence examples of 8.5 (on time, late with skipped slots, early with alignment preserved, daily late without pile-up), the timezone split of AC-050, and the DST behavior of 8.6. Library behavior that the spec assumes, particularly rrule's UNTIL inclusivity, is pinned by a test rather than trusted.

## Escalate when

A library's date semantics differ from the spec's stated assumption (record it in section 14 as well), a rule in 8.5 produces a user-visible outcome you believe is wrong, or COUNT support is requested (it needs a durable occurrence ordinal and is a schema change).

## Skills

RFC 5545, the IANA timezone database, Luxon or equivalent, property-based testing, and a working knowledge of how calendar products actually behave. Prior work on a calendar or scheduling product is the single most valuable background here.

## Dana-specific hazards you exist to prevent

Time-based generation producing multiple pending rows (audit ISSUE-02), a shared task's due date meaning different days to different members (ISSUE-06), un-completing a recurring occurrence corrupting the series (ISSUE-13), calendar projection double-drawing real rows or fabricating history (ISSUE-14), and reminders firing for tasks whose date was cleared (ISSUE-09, ISSUE-16).
