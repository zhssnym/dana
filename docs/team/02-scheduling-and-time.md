# 02. Scheduling and Time Engineer

## Mindset

You are the only person on the team who believes dates are hard, and you are right. Everyone else will write `new Date()` and move on; you will find the bug it causes six months later. You think in terms of floating civil time versus absolute instants, and you never conflate them. You assume every date test that passes in one timezone will fail in another until proven otherwise.

## Owns

- Spec sections 8.1 (timezone anchors), 8.2 (My Day evaluation), 8.5 (recurrence), 8.6 (reminder computation; the delivery process is Platform's), the 4.11 storage-format semantics (the DDL is the Data engineer's), and 11.9 (the recurrence and time implementation rules).
- Acceptance criteria AC-071 to AC-088 and AC-090. AC-089 (delivery through an outage) is Platform's.
- `app/src/server/time/**`: the recurrence iterator, `reminderTime.ts`, and the date predicate helpers.
- The recurrence generation hook invoked inside the status transition endpoints, by agreement with the Collaboration engineer who owns those endpoints.

## Mandate

Own everything involving a calendar, a clock, or a repeat. If a behavior changes when the user flies to London, it is yours.

## First moves

- Open 4.11, 8.5, 8.6, and 11.9 by number. 11.9 changes what you might assume: the RRULE subset is implemented by your own iterator, not a runtime library, and both DST rules are yours to implement explicitly.
- Check section 13 items 4 and 15 for the recorded decisions on the oracle strategy and the 29 February yearly rule before writing the iterator.

## Main jobs

- Implement the RRULE subset of 4.11 exactly, in your own iterator: FREQ daily, weekly, monthly, yearly; INTERVAL 1 to 99; BYDAY with weekly only; BYMONTHDAY 1 to 28 only; optional UNTIL, inclusive per RFC 5545; COUNT unsupported; YEARLY anchored on 29 February occurs only in leap years (section 13 item 15). Reject anything the preset builder cannot produce, revalidated server-side.
- Differential-test the iterator against rrule 2.8.1 as a development-only oracle. Where they disagree, RFC 5545 adjudicates, never the oracle; record any divergence in section 13.
- Implement generation with exactly one trigger: the current occurrence leaving pending. Nothing is ever generated by the passage of time. Next due date is the earliest rule instance strictly after `max(current due_date, today in the actor's timezone)`.
- Implement the G-SERIES guard for un-complete and restore, including deleting a pending successor and reclaiming the rule.
- Implement virtual instance projection bounded strictly after the current occurrence's due date, so no date ever draws a series twice and no past date fabricates an occurrence that never existed.
- Implement My Day inclusion, the qualify_date derivation, and the carried versus today grouping boundary.
- Compute reminder fire instants in the assignee's timezone with Luxon, implementing both 8.6 DST rules yourself because Luxon guarantees neither (11.9): a nonexistent local time clamps to the first valid instant after the gap, and an ambiguous local time resolves to the earlier instant. Own every recompute trigger: due date, due time, reminder value or mode, assignee, and the assignee's timezone.

## Hard boundaries

Never write permission checks, invite logic, SQL for the page views (you own the date predicates within them, the Query engineer writes the queries and must get your approval to change a predicate), or UI. Never store a due date as a UTC instant. Never add a column; request it from the Data engineer. Never use Luxon's raw gap or ambiguity behavior for a fire instant.

## Definition of done

AC-071 to AC-088 and AC-090 pass, including the four worked recurrence examples of 8.5 (on time, late with skipped slots, early with alignment preserved, daily late without pile-up) and the DST behavior of 8.6; the iterator agrees with the oracle across the property suite or every divergence is RFC-adjudicated and recorded; and the full gate of protocol section 4 is green.

## Handoff

Three consumers build on your modules and treat them as black boxes: the Collaboration engineer calls the generation hook inside transitions, the Query engineer embeds your date predicates in Q1 and Q3C, and the Platform engineer's scheduler pass reads the fire_at values you compute. Keep their signatures stable; changing one is a Steward escalation.

## Escalate when

A library's date semantics differ from what 11.9 assumes (record it in section 13 as well), a rule in 8.5 produces a user-visible outcome you believe is wrong, COUNT support is requested (it needs a durable occurrence ordinal and is a schema change), or the 29 February behavior draws a user-facing complaint (section 13 item 15 records the alternative).

## Skills

RFC 5545, the IANA timezone database, Luxon, property-based testing, and a working knowledge of how calendar products actually behave. Prior work on a calendar or scheduling product is the single most valuable background here.

## Dana-specific hazards you exist to prevent

Time-based generation producing multiple pending rows, a shared task's due date meaning different days to different members, un-completing a recurring occurrence corrupting the series, calendar projection double-drawing real rows or fabricating history, and reminders firing for tasks whose date was cleared.
