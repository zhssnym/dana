### 7.2 Settings — retired

**Retirement annotation, 2026-09-04, issue #113, on the owner's ruling.** This
subsection is retired. `GET /api/v1/me/settings` and `PATCH
/api/v1/me/settings` are gone from the application: the routes are deleted and
every method answers `404` in the 7.0 envelope, like any other path this
contract does not name. **[`7.23`](07.23-settings-bundle.md) is the settings
surface**, and it already was for everything but these three flags.

Section 7 is frozen and this is a breaking change to it rather than an
addition, so the ruling is written out. Two endpoints over one row of
`user_settings` was two contracts over one fact, which is what 4.19 asked not
to have; the client called `/settings` for the eight v2 fields and this pair
for three booleans nothing else read; and #152's write-up of 7.23 the same day
made the duplication impossible to leave unstated. The retirement ships
**after** its client half (#150) and in the same deploy, which is the
expand-and-contract order this change needed: a browser holding a bundle from
before the deploy only ever prefetches this endpoint, and swallows the 404.

**What is retired is the pair of endpoints and not the data.** The three
columns `show_important`, `show_all` and `show_done` stay stored and nothing is
migrated. They remain the record for a person's pins until that person first
writes a pin list: while `user_settings.pinned_views` is null, `GET /settings`
derives the list from these three, per 4.19 and 7.23's "pins before anybody has
chosen". **So a v1 account's sidebar is unchanged by this**, which is the whole
reason the columns are not dropped here. Dropping them is a later contract
migration, once nothing reads them, and a separate ruling.

**And they are still readable.** Frozen 7.1's `GET /me` answers them under its
`settings` key, untouched by this and still pinned by AC-116. What went is
every way of *writing* them: no route in this application now updates those
three columns.

## What this replaces

The retired text, kept because it is what the criteria and the client were
built against, and because a retirement that deletes its own subject leaves
nobody able to check what was retired:

> - GET /me/settings. { show_important, show_all, show_done }. - PATCH
>   /me/settings. Any subset of the three booleans. Hidden pages are a
>   navigation preference only; no data changes. 200.
>
> Response body, escalated from the P2S opening session and entered by owner
> ruling: PATCH /me/settings answers the GET /me/settings object, the same
> fresh-GET convention 7.1 carries on PATCH /me and 7.4 carries on each of its
> mutations.

The `calendar_zoom` annotation of 2026-09-04 (issues #140 and #142) also stood
here, and it was the first time section 7 named the bundle at all. It said in
terms that writing that endpoint up properly was separate work; #152 did that
in 7.23 the same day, and 7.23 carries `calendar_zoom` as contract rather than
as annotation. The rulings it recorded — Imran's on #140, that the calendar's
zoom follows the account rather than the browser, and the owner's that it rides
on the existing bundle path rather than an endpoint of its own — are unaffected
by this retirement and live on there.

## The criteria

AC-119 to AC-123 were this subsection's and all five moved with it; 12.3
records each one's disposition and the reason. In short: AC-119, AC-120 and
AC-121 are **rewritten** against the bundle, because the rules they pinned are
7.23's word for word — the column defaults, any subset including the empty one
with absent fields unchanged, and a wrong value answering 422 naming the field
and writing nothing. AC-122 and AC-123 are **retired**: the first because
AC-379 already asserts cross-account isolation on the surviving endpoint and
says so by name, the second because the behaviour it forbade — a toggle write
touching a row outside `user_settings` — is unreachable now that there is no
toggle write.
