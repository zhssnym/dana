### 9.2 Landing and login

Public page: one page describing the shipping product, collaborative to-do lists in
shareable folders; a single "Continue with Google" control; privacy and terms links. The
page shows the product as the product renders it rather than as illustration. After
callback, first-run lands on Desk at /app per 9.1's route table; pending invites, when any
exist, gate the application first as 9.8 describes.

**The landing page describes what Dana does, never what it might do.** Every capability
the page names must be one a person can use on the day they read it, and no copy may
promise, imply or preview a capability Dana does not ship. This is stated as a rule
because it is broken by addition rather than by omission: no assertion about what the page
does contain can catch a sentence that should not be there, and the pressure to write one
is highest exactly when the product is smallest. Its named instance, and the one AC-159
asserts, is that no copy anywhere on this page claims AI, which section 1 says v2 does not
have and will not.

Copy and composition are the design record's, adopted by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-24,
and are not transcribed here. This section fixes the rules copy is written under rather
than the sentences it is written as, and that is the change the ruling made. A landing
page is marketing, and marketing moves; a specification carrying its sentences would hold
a second copy of the design record and drift from it on the first edit, silently, because
no criterion can compare two passages of prose and say which one is stale. What a
criterion can hold is a rule, so the rules are what this section keeps:

- No capability claimed that Dana does not ship, AI named as the instance above (AC-159).
  - Exactly one "Continue with Google" control on the page. The same call to action in a
  hero and again in a closing block is two, and AC-159 counts the page rather than the
  hero (AC-159). - Privacy and terms links (AC-159). - The page names folders and says the
  product is shared (AC-159).

One string stays normative here, because it states a fact about what Dana takes rather
than a claim about what Dana does, and its accuracy is a privacy obligation rather than an
editorial choice. Under the sign-in control: "Dana requests only your Google profile and
email for sign-in, sharing, and invitations."

**The Privacy page says the data can be taken away, added 2026-09-04 for issue #162.**
DES-032's approved copy tells a person what Dana stores, what it is used for, and that an
account and its data can be deleted, and it said nothing about getting the data out,
because when it was approved there was nothing to get it out with. 7.22 is that surface and
9.7 carries the control, so the page gains a section, "Taking your data with you", holding
one sentence: "You can download everything Dana holds about you at any time, as a single
JSON file, from your Settings page." It sits immediately before "Deleting your data",
those two being one pair in the order a person does them, and it names Settings rather than
the endpoint, an address being no use to somebody reading a privacy policy. 10.9 records
why it is owed: the Google APIs Terms of Service section 3(d) measures Dana against how
easily Google itself hands the same data over, which is a portability obligation rather
than an erasure one, and a page describing only deletion answers the wrong half of it.
AC-404 states the sentence beside the control, deliberately in one criterion — states it,
rather than standing over it, its test file having gone in the 2026-09-20 cut: **this is
the one sentence on a public page that claims a capability**, which the rule above admits
only while Dana ships it, so a criterion that could pass with the sentence and no control
would be the wrong criterion.

The sign-in control is a link and not a button, which is behaviour rather than
presentation and is stated because the design renders a button. It carries the viewer's
detected timezone to the authorisation endpoint of section 7, which is how a first session
gets a timezone before any row exists to hold one; a button with a click handler could
carry it too, and a link is what makes the destination visible, bookmarkable and reachable
with scripting unavailable. AC-159 admits either role deliberately and does not settle
this.

Where the page animates a heading, the accessible name is the settled sentence and the
animation is decorative. The adopted landing rotates one verb inside its headline and
renders a single fixed sentence to assistive technology, which is what 9.1's addressing
sentence requires of any name a person is read: a heading whose text depends on when it is
sampled has no name at all.

The copy that shipped at P8 was the human owner's own, written on 2026-08-11 and recorded
as closing section 13 item 14; the designer's replaced it on the owner's ruling of
2026-08-13. Both were tables of sentences in this section. Item 14 closes a third time on
2026-08-24 against the form above rather than against any wording, which is what that item
was always protecting: it recorded the no-AI rule and not who held the pen, and that rule
now has a section stating it and a criterion asserting it, neither of which depends on the
copy staying still.

Unverified-email rejection page (10.1; AC-008): rendered by the callback in place of
establishing a session when Google reports the email unverified; no rows are created.
Copy, normative like all copy in this section: heading "We can't sign you in yet"; body
"Your Google email address isn't verified. Dana uses your email for sharing and invites,
so it needs Google to confirm it first. Verify your email with Google, then try again.";
one action, a link labeled "Back to Dana", returning to the landing page.

Claimed-email rejection page (10.1; AC-346), added 2026-09-03 on Imran's ruling against
issue #17: rendered by the callback in place of establishing a session when Google
reports a verified email that already belongs to a different live account; no rows are
created and nothing is joined. Copy, normative like all copy in this section: heading
"This email already has an account"; body "An account already uses this email address,
and Dana never joins two accounts together. Sign in with the Google account you used the
first time. If that still does not get you in, write to support.danadana@gmail.com and a
person will answer."; two actions, a link labeled "Back to Dana" returning to the landing
page, and a quieter one labeled "Email support" opening a message to the same address.
The address is the one Settings and the legal pages already give (issue #23) and is one
constant, `src/shared/support.ts`, rather than a second address written out here.

The ruling's three parts are the body's three sentences, in its order: an account already
uses this email, try your original Google account, and here is support if you still cannot
get in. That is why the sentence count is fixed and the sentences are not merged.

**Why this page names the fact rather than saying only that sign-in failed.** The
question was whether telling somebody "an account already uses this email" discloses the
existence of an account. It does not, in the only sense that can hurt: nobody reaches this
page without completing the whole handshake of 11.6 and without Google asserting that this
verified address is theirs, so the only person who can be shown the sentence is the
mailbox's owner, and an attacker who wants to ask the question has first to hold a Google
account at the address, at which point they can read the mailbox. Against that, the
refusal is the one dead end in the product: there is no password to reset and no second
factor to try, and a person holding the wrong Google account has no other way to be told
so. The reasoning is repeated in `auth/pages.ts` beside the copy, deliberately, because a
page that says less is the obvious "safer" edit and the next reader needs the argument in
front of them.

Both rejection pages answer 403 and neither establishes a session. Every other sign-in
refusal still returns to the landing page carrying ?signin=failed and says 9.1's global
failure sentence: this section gives the callback these two pages and no others.
