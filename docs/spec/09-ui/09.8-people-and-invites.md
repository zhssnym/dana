### 9.8 People and invites

People screen (reachable from the account menu in the sidebar footer, or from Settings; it
was a footer link of its own until the polish round of 2026-08-30 moved it into that menu;
nothing in the roster says human, an entry being an entry whatever it is, L-096): roster
list with remove control ("Remove connection? You can still see shared folders; you'll
just need a fresh invite to share new ones."); bare invite by email, refused when the
address is already a connection ("You're already connected to this person. They're in your
People list.", the 409 of 7.5 entered by owner ruling on 2026-08-09). Invite gate: when
Q12 is non-empty, the pending invites render in place of the application rather than
inside it. It is one screen, headed "Invitations", carrying each invite as a row reading
"Hassan invited you to Wedding" (or "Hassan invited you to connect") with Accept and
Decline; the sidebar, the page beneath and the rest of the chrome are not rendered.
Answering the last invite ends the gate, and accepting a folder invite lands inside that
folder. The gate is the only surface that lists pending invites, and no page inside the
application carries them. Assignment chip: rows and the form show "Invite pending:
sara@example.com" while an assignment-invite is outstanding.

Where that list lives was ruled at the second P8 change control on 2026-08-11 and is
changed here by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-13, recording the designer's DES-081. The
earlier ruling made it a section of My Day above everything else on that page, choosing
that over two rejected readings: a band above every page, which would have been global
chrome 9.1 did not name, and a surface appearing once after callback, which would have
left anyone reloading before answering with no invite in front of them. The gate answers
the second objection the same way, being a standing condition on the account rather than a
moment after a callback, and it answers the first by not being chrome at all. What changed
is the weighing of the first: this section says the invites render before anything else,
and a section at the top of one page is before anything else only for a person who happens
to be looking at that page, while a gate is what that phrase plainly says. The cost,
stated because it is real, is that a person holding an unanswered invite cannot reach the
rest of Dana without answering or declining it, which is the thing a gate is and the thing
a section is not.

The gate is a region with no name, which is a decision and not an omission, and it is the
earlier ruling's reasoning unchanged. 9.1's sections rule takes a region's name from the
heading this section gives it or from the datum it is generated from, and this list has
neither: it is one list rather than one per folder. Its rows are addressed the way a task
row's controls are addressed, from the row's own sentence, which is what that sentence is
for: "Hassan invited you to Wedding" tells a reader and a test which Accept belongs to
which invite. AC-172 states the gate and is rewritten by RFC 0002, its present text
describing the section this ruling replaces. It is a decision and not a proof: its test
file went in the 2026-09-20 cut.

The People screen carries no invite list, corrected at the P8R mid-package change control
on 2026-08-13 on the Adversarial Test Engineer's escalation, which found that it could not
be asserted because it could not be reached. When the gate arrived at acceptance, the
sentence giving the People screen the same list was carried over from the ruling the gate
replaced, and the two do not fit: the gate renders at every /app path while Q12 is
non-empty, /app/people is an /app path, so the one page that was to hold the list is the
one page unreachable exactly while the list would have anything in it. The sentence
described a surface no person could ever see. Two repairs were available and the other is
recorded so it is not reintroduced: exempting /app/people from the gate would have kept
the list at the cost of a hole in a gate whose entire point is that invites come first,
and it would have given a person two places to answer an invite where the ruling's
argument was that they should have one that finds them. The list goes instead, which costs
nothing, because what it was for was "where a person returns to an invite they have not
answered" and the gate is now that place by construction: a person who reloads, or who
navigates anywhere in the application, arrives at it.
