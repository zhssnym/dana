### 9.8 People and invites

People, a section of Settings (`app/src/client/components/settings/people.tsx`, 9.7),
at /app/settings#people; the old /app/people redirects there:
the roster with a remove control behind a confirmation saying shared folders stay visible
and a fresh invite is needed to share new ones; and a bare invite by email, refused when
the address is already a connection (7.5's 409). Nothing in the roster says human; an
entry is an entry whatever it is. The People section carries no incoming invite list (see below).

Assignment chip: rows and the card show "Invite pending: {email}" while an
assignment-invite is outstanding.

**Invite gate** (`app/src/client/pages/InviteGate.tsx`). While Q12 is non-empty, the pending invites
render in place of the application rather than inside it: one screen headed
"Invitations", each invite a row reading "Hassan invited you to Wedding" (or "Hassan
invited you to connect") with Accept and Decline; the sidebar, the page beneath and the
rest of the chrome are not rendered. Answering the last invite ends the gate; accepting a
folder invite lands inside that folder. The gate is the only surface that lists pending
invites.

Why a gate: invites render before anything else, and only a gate is "before anything
else" for every person on every page; a standing condition on the account also catches
someone who reloads before answering. The cost, accepted, is that a person with an
unanswered invite cannot reach the rest of Dana without answering or declining it.

The gate is a region with no name, deliberately: it has neither a heading of its own kind
nor a datum it is generated from (it is one list, not one per folder). Its rows are
addressed from their own sentence, which tells a reader and a test which Accept belongs to
which invite.

No invite list elsewhere, and in particular none on the People screen: the gate renders at
every /app path while Q12 is non-empty, so a list at /app/people could never be seen while
it had anything in it. Exempting /app/people from the gate is the rejected alternative — it
would hole a gate whose point is that invites come first, and give a person two places to
answer an invite.
