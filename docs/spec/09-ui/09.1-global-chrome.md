### 9.1 Global chrome

The shell is `app/src/client/shell/` (`app/src/client/shell/app-shell.tsx`, `app/src/client/shell/app-sidebar.tsx`);
what each part draws is there. The router is the only record of where the viewer stands. The rules it is held to:

- **Sidebar.** The navigation landmark and the application's only side chrome. Top to
  bottom: one head row holding only "Search" (a quiet row whose Ctrl K / ⌘K keycap shows on
  hover) that opens the palette, a small square "Notifications" bell (a dot while any are
  unread) and "Collapse sidebar", set apart by one divider; Today, Upcoming (the Scheduled page) and Assigned
  (the Assignments page), each a link; the Library; the footer. No logo, no Desk entry
  (Desk is the Library's first folder), and no other page entries, disclosure or saved
  views.
- **Collapse.** Collapsing hides the sidebar entirely, with no icon rail, and makes it
  inert; a small "Expand sidebar" control at the page's top left brings it back. Ctrl+B /
  Cmd+B toggles either way. When the control that held focus disappears, focus moves to the
  toggle now showing; focus elsewhere stays where it is.
- **Library.** A "Library" heading with one "+" named "New page", resting visible because a
  touchscreen has no hover, which makes an Untitled top-level page in one press and opens it
  with its title selected; it has no menu. Import is in a page's "…" menu and in the
  sidebar's own context menu. Below it
  the Desk folder first, then the viewer's shelves and folders as one ordered list; the
  order is the viewer's own, seeded alphabetically and rearranged by dragging (rows
  reorder, move onto and off shelves, shelf headings drag as rows). A closed shelf is never
  sprung open by a drag hovering it; the drop reads "Into {collection}". Pages nest: a sub-page
  sits indented under its parent, with no tree lines, and a parent with sub-pages folds from a
  chevron over its mark on hover. Dropping a page on the middle of another page's row puts it
  inside; dropping between sub-pages moves it there; dropping beside a top-level row makes it
  top level. A page never drops onto itself or anything under it. A row's hover "+" ("Add a
  page inside") makes an Untitled sub-page and opens it. A page whose parent the viewer is not
  a member of is top level for that viewer. The word for
  a group of folders is "collection" in every dialog, menu, toast and confirm (the data
  model, routes and storage keep "shelf"), and a shelf may be empty.
- **Folder rows.** A row shows the folder's mark and name only, no count. Its "More
  actions" button appears on hover in space the row always reserves, so nothing shifts,
  and is always visible on touch. Its menu is what makes sense on a row: Rename, Copy link,
  Duplicate, then Leave page… or Delete page by role; Desk has no Delete. History,
  Import as sub-page… and Export as template are in the page's own "…". A page with no icon of its own shows the
  plain page glyph, never a folder. A page
  moves, into another page or onto a collection, by dragging its row.
- **Collection (shelf) headings.** The icon is the fold toggle, turning into a chevron on hover in the
  same box; the name links to the shelf page. Its "More actions" menu: Rename collection, Copy
  link, Delete collection… (which asks to confirm).
- **Context menus.** Right-click on a folder row or a shelf heading opens the same menu
  as its "More actions". Right-click anywhere else on the sidebar (empty space, the lists,
  the Library heading, the footer) opens a small menu at the pointer: "New page" and
  "Import…" (a Dana file, imported at the Library's top level).
- **Footer.** Two direct rows, "Settings" and "Trash", each a link, and nothing else. The
  footer is Settings' one home in the chrome.
- **Account.** The sidebar has no account button or menu; Sign out is in Settings' Account
  section (9.7). People is a section of Settings; the sidebar does not link it, and
  /app/people still answers.
- **Narrow widths.** Below 768px the sidebar is a drawer opened by "Menu" on a phone bar
  that holds "Menu" and "Search" and nothing else; the drawer holds everything the sidebar
  does except the Search field, the head row with its Notifications bell included. It closes on navigation, on
  its backdrop and on Escape. At that width the task surface of 9.3 takes the whole width
  in every mode and draws no resize handle.
- **Window bar.** Belongs to the window, not the page: the breadcrumb at the left, the page's controls at the right (the object's overflow), and "Update available, reload" when a new build is waiting. Past the breadcrumb's last level it never repeats the page title. A page adds a control
  there when the control belongs to the window rather than the body (Assignments' scope,
  9.5; a folder's member faces and Share, 9.6). A page fills it by portalling into it, so
  it keeps its own state while drawing into shell chrome (`app/src/client/components/window-bar.tsx`).
- **Breadcrumb.** At the window bar's left end: "Go back" and "Go forward" arrows, each
  disabled when there is no entry in that direction within Dana and hidden on a phone; then
  the path, levels separated by "/". On a page it is the ancestors the viewer may read, root
  first, then the page; on a task, the task's page trail then the task's title. Every level
  but the last is a link. More than three levels show the first, a "…" whose menu holds the
  hidden pages, then the last two; a phone shows only the last two. Today, Upcoming,
  Assigned, Settings, Trash and a collection show just their own name.
- **View menu** (`app/src/client/views/FilterControl.tsx`, `ViewMenu`). One text button
  "View" beside a list page's title, opening at most five items, each a word: Layout
  (only where the page can honestly draw more than a list: board on Assigned, calendar on
  Upcoming), Group by, Sort, Show completed. It is the page's only view or filter control;
  there is no filter panel, no chips and no saved views. The choice belongs to the page and
  is kept on return, never carried to another page. `?mode=` still names a layout.
  Show completed is the one way back to finished work on every list (Today, Upcoming,
  Assigned, a folder): completed rows show checked and muted, and abandoned rows stay in
  place, struck and muted; off, neither shows. On Today, a row dated later sits under
  "Started" (its Schedule has begun) or "Added to Today" (pulled in), never under "Today".
- Grouping from the View menu replaces the page's own arrangement rather than nesting
  inside it.
- **Capture.** Every list ends in a "New task" row, an always-visible text box; N or C
  focuses it when the person is not typing, Enter makes the task and keeps the box for
  the next. In board view every column captures, and in the calendar a day cell opens its
  own inline "New task" line dated that day. There is no create dialog and no floating
  button: on a phone the "New task" row stays pinned to the bottom of the screen while the
  list scrolls.
- **Keyboard shortcuts.** `?` (when not typing) opens the "Keyboard shortcuts" sheet, which
  lists only keys that are bound; search offers the same sheet as "Keyboard shortcuts". Every capture uses 9.3's entry defaults for
  where it stands, so a task made on a page appears on that page; in a hand-ordered list
  the new task lands at its foot, in typing order. A folder page captures through its
  canvas (9.6) instead.
- **Undo** (`app/src/client/components/undo-bar.tsx`, `app/src/client/shell/undo.ts`). One bar for the window, naming
  the act it takes back, bottom centre (above the floating button on a phone), for five
  seconds, Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z; no redo. Each act registers its
  own inverse, and undo issues the inverse as a new mutation: no write is ever held back
  waiting for the window to close. The bar's wording is this section's and 4.13's event is
  written server-side in the mutation's transaction; neither is derived from the other.
- **The mark** (`app/src/client/components/dana-mark.tsx`). Inline SVG everywhere it appears. Its rest
  state is the whole logo, so a mark that never animates is simply the logo; each
  instance's mask id is unique; `prefers-reduced-motion` turns motion off, and a region
  may switch icon motion off wholesale.
- **Object acts** (`app/src/client/lib/object-acts.ts`). Every kind of thing declares its acts once and
  every overflow menu draws them through one component, so where a person stands never
  decides what they may do. Five groups in fixed order — reach, change, copy, stop,
  destroy — destroy last behind a separator; stopping (Abandon) and destroying (Delete)
  are never one group. An act with no handler is not drawn; a surface may add rendered
  items of its own above the contract's.
- **Panels.** A side panel takes room from the page rather than covering it; its width is
  one value the shell reads, shared by every panel, so two cannot overlap.
- **Library drag** (`app/src/client/shell/library-drag.ts`, `app/src/client/lib/use-sortable-list.ts`). Pointer-driven
  rather than HTML5 drag-and-drop, so it works on touch and auto-scrolls; the same list
  sorts by keyboard, the handle a real button.
- **Failure.** Offline, a global banner persists and writes fail with the offline toast;
  any other failed request shows the global error toast; a 409 shows 5.1's state-machine
  copy verbatim, and a stale page-block write (4.17) the same way. Words:
  `app/src/client/components/state-patterns.tsx`, `app/src/client/state/toasts.ts`.

Routes. Paths: `app/src/client/routes.ts`. /app redirects to /app/today, so a person with
nothing else asked for lands on Today (10.6 fixes the installed app's start_url at /app).
Desk is an ordinary folder page at its folder path, and /app/desk redirects there. The
retired paths /app/planned, /app/tasks, /app/all, /app/anytime, /app/important,
/app/done and /app/abandoned redirect, replacing the history entry, rather than 404,
because bookmarks exist; finished and abandoned work is reached through a folder's "Show
completed", not a page. A hidden (unpinned) page still renders at its URL.
/app/tasks/:id opens the task in the viewer's preferred surface over the page beneath; an
inaccessible or absent id answers with the 404 copy below, under 10.2.

Addressing. Every control this section names is reachable by its accessible role and its
name below; that is what a screen reader announces and what a test addresses.

| Element | Role |
| - | - |
| The sidebar | A navigation landmark |
| The page entries, each folder row and each shelf heading's name | Links |
| A task row | A list item, or a row |
| The task panel | A complementary region named for the task; on a phone, the page |
| A toggle switch | A switch |
| A calendar day | A grid cell within a grid |
| A board column | A named region; its cards, list items or rows |
| A view option in the strip | A radio within a radiogroup, checked when current |

| Control | Name |
| - | - |

| The inline retry control | "Try again" |

An ellipsis in a label means one thing: a further step (a confirmation or a picker)
follows before anything happens. An act that takes effect on the press carries none.

Regions. Every section a page's structure names is a named region, named by its heading
or, where it has none, by the datum it is generated from: one per shelf in the sidebar;
Today's "Carried over", "Today", and "Done" while completed tasks are shown; one per Upcoming ladder rung; one per
person on Assigned, "Unassigned" last; one per user section on a
folder page, plus "Done" and "Sharing panel"; one per board column, named by its value.
A collapsible section is a named region plus a control carrying that name, whose expanded
state is what open and collapsed mean.

Inline state copy (`app/src/client/components/ui/copy.ts`, `app/src/client/components/page/PageStates.tsx`):

| State | Copy |
| - | - |
| A read that failed, beside the thing that failed | "Couldn't load this. Nothing was changed.", with "Try again" |
| A resource that answers 404 (10.2) | "Not found. This may have been deleted, or it may never have been yours to see." |
| A response carrying truncated (section 6's cap rule) | "This list is long, so only the first part is shown. Narrow it down to see the rest." |
| A write refused by the storage quota (10.3) | "Out of space. Delete some files in Settings, or remove attachments you no longer need." |

The 404 sentence never distinguishes deleted from never-visible, which is 10.2's
non-disclosure rule said aloud. The inline failed-read line is the page's own words and
never the global toast's: a page saying "Nothing was saved" where nothing was being saved
tells a person something false about their own data.
