### 9.1 Global chrome

The shell is `app/src/client/shell/` (`app/src/client/shell/app-shell.tsx`, `app/src/client/shell/app-sidebar.tsx`);
what each part draws is there. The router is the only record of where the viewer stands. The rules it is held to:

- **Sidebar.** The navigation landmark and the application's only side chrome. Top to
  bottom: a "Focus" heading over the viewer's Focus lists (Today, Upcoming (the Scheduled
  page) and Assigned by default), each a link, drawn exactly as the Library's heading, with
  nothing above it; the Library; the footer. Assigned is drawn only when the viewer belongs
  to a page that has another member, because for someone working alone it would list every
  task. The Focus heading's "+" ("Add a list") offers the lists not shown: Important,
  Overdue, No date and All open (9.5), and any hidden default. A Focus row drags to reorder,
  and its "…" offers "Hide" for a default and "Remove" for an added list; either comes back
  from the "+". The choice and order are the account's (4.10's `focus_lists`), not the
  browser's. Each Focus list wears its own icon and colour (4.10's `focus_appearance`) on its
  row, its page head, its tab and in search, and its page may wear a cover (4.20). No logo,
  no Desk entry (Desk is the Library's first folder), and no other page entries or
  disclosure.
- **Collapse.** Collapsing hides the sidebar entirely, with no icon rail, and makes it
  inert; a small "Expand sidebar" control in the window's bottom-left corner, at the edge the sidebar opens from, brings it back. Ctrl+B /
  Cmd+B toggles either way. When the control that held focus disappears, focus moves to the
  toggle now showing; focus elsewhere stays where it is.
- **Library.** A "Library" heading with one "+" named "New project", resting visible because
  a touchscreen has no hover. It opens two items, "New project" (an Untitled project, opened
  with its title selected) and "Import project…" (a Dana file, drawn with the plain import
  glyph); the sidebar's own context menu holds the same two. Below it
  the Desk folder first, then the viewer's shelves and projects as one ordered list; the
  order is the viewer's own, seeded alphabetically and rearranged by dragging (rows
  reorder, move onto and off shelves, shelf headings drag as rows). A closed shelf is never
  sprung open by a drag hovering it; the drop reads "Into {collection}". **A Library row is a
  project and only opens it**: it has no fold and draws no sub-pages, whose home is the
  project's tabs, and a drop never puts one row inside another. By keyboard the list is one
  Tab stop: ↑/↓ move between rows, Enter opens, F2 renames, Shift+F10 opens the row's menu.
  A page whose parent the viewer is not a member of is top level for that viewer. The word for
  a group of folders is "collection" in every dialog, menu, toast and confirm (the data
  model, routes and storage keep "shelf"), and a shelf may be empty.
- **Project rows.** A row shows the project's mark and name only, no count. Its "More
  actions" button appears on hover in space the row always reserves, so nothing shifts,
  and is always visible on touch. Its menu is what makes sense on a row: Open in new tab,
  Rename, Copy link, Duplicate, Archive work, then Leave work… or Delete work by role. Each runs in
  place: Rename turns the row into its name field, Copy link says "Link copied", Duplicate
  opens the copy, and Archive work or Delete work (to Trash) offers Undo. Leave keeps the page on screen
  unless it was that project, which goes to Today. History and Export project are in the
  project's own "…". The Desk's row has no menu. A page with no icon of its own shows the
  plain page glyph, never a folder. A project moves onto a collection by dragging its row.
- **Collection (shelf) headings.** The icon is the fold toggle, turning into a chevron on hover in the
  same box; the name links to the shelf page. Its "More actions" menu: Rename collection, Copy
  link, Delete collection… (which asks to confirm).
- **Context menus.** Right-click on a folder row or a shelf heading opens the same menu
  as its "More actions". Right-click anywhere else on the sidebar (empty space, the lists,
  the Library heading, the footer) opens a small menu at the pointer: "New page" and
  "Import…" (a Dana file, imported at the Library's top level).
- **Footer.** One row of square icon buttons of one size, no text: "Search" (opens the
  palette; its tooltip names the Ctrl K / ⌘K shortcut), the "Notifications" bell (a dot
  while any are unread; its list opens above it), "Settings" and "Trash" (links), and
  "Collapse sidebar" at the far right. Each has a tooltip naming it and a tint on hover, so
  the only text in the sidebar is the viewer's lists and pages. The footer is Settings' one
  home in the chrome. With the sidebar collapsed, "Expand sidebar", the same icon, sits in
  the window\'s bottom-left corner.
- **Account.** The sidebar has no account button or menu; Sign out is in Settings' Account
  section (9.7). People is a section of Settings; the sidebar does not link it, and
  /app/people still answers.
- **Narrow widths.** Below 768px the sidebar is a drawer opened by "Menu" on a phone bar
  that holds "Menu" and "Search" and nothing else; the drawer holds everything the sidebar
  does, the footer row included. It closes on navigation, on
  its backdrop and on Escape.
- **Workspace tabs** (`app/src/client/shell/TabStrip.tsx`, `app/src/client/shell/tabs.ts`).
  On a laptop a quiet strip above the window bar holds "Go back" and "Go forward", then the
  places the viewer has open as tabs ("Open tabs"), each wearing its place's mark and title.
  Ordinary navigation retargets the current tab; "Open in new tab" on a page, collection or
  task menu, or the strip's "+" ("Open tab", a search over places, pages, collections and
  tasks, with "New page" below), adds one. A task opens as its task page (9.3). Tabs drag to
  reorder and close with their "×" or a middle click; closing the last one lands on Desk.
  The set is session state, never stored. A phone draws no strip. These are not a page's
  Purpose / Work / Results / Discussion tabs.
- **Window bar.** Belongs to the window, not the page: the breadcrumb at the left, the page's controls at the right (the object's overflow), and "Update available, reload" when a new build is waiting. Past the breadcrumb's last level it never repeats the page title. A page adds a control
  there when the control belongs to the window rather than the body (Assignments' scope,
  9.5; a folder's member faces and Share, 9.6). A page fills it by portalling into it, so
  it keeps its own state while drawing into shell chrome (`app/src/client/components/window-bar.tsx`).
- **Breadcrumb.** At the window bar's left end: the path (the "Go back" and "Go forward"
  arrows, each disabled when there is no entry in that direction within Dana, sit on the
  workspace tab strip and are hidden on a phone), levels separated by "/". On a page it is the ancestors the viewer may read, root
  first, then the page; on a task, the task's page trail then the task's title. Every level
  but the last is a link. More than three levels show the first, a "…" whose menu holds the
  hidden pages, then the last two; a phone shows only the last two. Today, Upcoming,
  Assigned, Settings, Trash and a collection show just their own name.
- **No view menu.** A list page draws its one arrangement, with no Group by, Sort, Label
  or "Show completed" control and no filter panel or saved views. Upcoming's one choice,
  list or calendar, is a single button beside its title ("Show as calendar", "Show as
  list"); `?mode=` still names it. Finished work is seen in place on its page's thread
  (9.6), never gathered by a control. On Today, a started task (a start date today or
  earlier) sits under "Started" unless it is overdue; overdue work, started or not,
  sits under "Carried over". A row dated later that has not started sits under "Added to
  Today" (pulled in), never under "Today". A row under a heading that names its day
  (Tomorrow, Saturday on Upcoming) shows only its time; an overdue date is in the late
  colour on every screen.
- **Keyboard in a task list** (Today, Upcoming, Assigned). The
  list is one Tab stop; the arrows move a visible focus between tasks, Enter opens the focused task and Space completes it, focus then moving to the
  task that took its place.
- **Capture.** Every list ends in a "New task" row, an always-visible text box; N or C
  focuses it when the person is not typing, Enter makes the task and keeps the box for
  the next. In the calendar a day cell opens its
  own inline "New task" line dated that day. There is no create dialog and no floating
  button: on a phone the "New task" row stays pinned to the bottom of the screen while the
  list scrolls.
- **Keyboard shortcuts.** `?` (when not typing) opens the "Keyboard shortcuts" sheet, which
  lists only keys that are bound, grouped by where they work (anywhere, a list of tasks, an
  open task, the sidebar); search offers the same sheet as "Keyboard shortcuts". Ctrl+B /
  Cmd+B shows or hides the sidebar except while typing, where it is bold. Every capture uses 9.3's entry defaults for
  where it stands, so a task made on a page appears on that page; in a hand-ordered list
  the new task lands at its foot, in typing order. A folder page captures through its
  canvas (9.6) instead.
- **Undo** (`app/src/client/components/undo-bar.tsx`, `app/src/client/shell/undo.ts`). One bar for the window, naming
  the act it takes back, bottom centre in the one notice column it shares with toasts
  (`app/src/client/state/ToastRegion.tsx`), with no dismiss control, for five
  seconds, Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z; no redo. Each act registers its
  own inverse, and undo issues the inverse as a new mutation: no write is ever held back
  waiting for the window to close. The bar's wording is this section's and 4.13's event is
  written server-side in the mutation's transaction; neither is derived from the other.
- **The mark** (`app/src/client/components/dana-mark.tsx`). Inline SVG everywhere it appears. Its rest
  state is the whole logo, so a mark that never animates is simply the logo; each
  instance's mask id is unique; `prefers-reduced-motion` turns motion off, and a region
  may switch icon motion off wholesale.
- **Object acts** (`app/src/client/lib/object-acts.ts`). Every kind of thing declares its acts once and
  every overflow menu draws them through one component, so where a person stands never
  decides what they may do. Five groups in fixed order — reach, change, copy, stop,
  destroy — destroy last behind a separator; stopping (Drop) and destroying (Delete)
  are never one group. An act with no handler is not drawn; a surface may add rendered
  items of its own above the contract's.
- **Panels.** A side panel takes room from the page rather than covering it; its width is
  one value the shell reads, shared by every panel, so two cannot overlap.
- **Library drag** (`app/src/client/shell/library-drag.ts`, `app/src/client/lib/use-sortable-list.ts`). Pointer-driven
  rather than HTML5 drag-and-drop, so it works on touch and auto-scrolls; the same list
  sorts by keyboard, the handle a real button.
- **Failure.** Offline, a global notice persists in the notice column and writes fail
  with the offline toast;
  any other failed request shows the global error toast; a 409 shows 5.1's state-machine
  copy verbatim, and a stale page-block write (4.17) the same way. Words:
  `app/src/client/components/state-patterns.tsx`, `app/src/client/state/toasts.ts`.

Routes. Paths: `app/src/client/routes.ts`. /app redirects to /app/today, so a person with
nothing else asked for lands on Today (10.6 fixes the installed app's start_url at /app).
Desk is an ordinary folder page at its folder path, and /app/desk redirects there. The
retired paths /app/planned, /app/tasks, /app/all, /app/anytime, /app/important,
/app/done and /app/abandoned redirect, replacing the history entry, rather than 404,
because bookmarks exist; finished and dropped work is seen in place on its page's thread,
not on a page of its own. A hidden (unpinned) page still renders at its URL.
/app/tasks/:id is the task's one page (9.3); an inaccessible or absent id answers with the 404 copy below, under 10.2.

Addressing. Every control this section names is reachable by its accessible role and its
name below; that is what a screen reader announces and what a test addresses.

| Element | Role |
| - | - |
| The sidebar | A navigation landmark |
| The page entries, each folder row and each shelf heading's name | Links |
| A task row | A list item, or a row |
| A toggle switch | A switch |
| A calendar day | A grid cell within a grid |

| Control | Name |
| - | - |
| The sidebar footer's search button and the phone bar's search control | "Search" |
| The inline new-task row at a list's end (focused by N or C when not typing) | "New task" |
| Every overflow menu (a folder's, a shelf's, the task page's; a row has none) | "More actions" |
| The two controls of every confirmation | "Continue" and "Cancel" (deleting goes to Trash with Undo and asks nothing) |
| The inline retry control | "Try again" |
| A task's completion control | "Complete task" when open, "Mark as not done" when complete |
| The Library's new-thing control | "New page" |
| The acts of making elsewhere | "New page", "New task" |
| The breadcrumb's arrows | "Go back" and "Go forward" |
| The sidebar footer's links | "Settings", "Trash" |
| The phone bar's opener | "Menu" |
| The important star | "Important" when on, "Mark as important" when off |
| The sidebar's notifications bell | "Notifications", suffixed ", N unread" while any are |
| The read-all control inside it | "Mark all read" |
| A folder's member exit | "Leave project…" |
| The sidebar's hide and show controls | "Collapse sidebar" / "Expand sidebar"; "Close sidebar" in the phone drawer |
| The sidebar's resize handle | "Resize sidebar" |

An ellipsis in a label means one thing: a further step (a confirmation or a picker)
follows before anything happens. An act that takes effect on the press carries none.

Regions. Every section a page's structure names is a named region, named by its heading
or, where it has none, by the datum it is generated from: one per shelf in the sidebar;
Today's "Carried over" and "Today"; one per Upcoming ladder rung; one per
person on Assigned, "Unassigned" last; one per user section on a
folder page, plus "Sharing panel".
A collapsible section is a named region plus a control carrying that name, whose expanded
state is what open and collapsed mean.

Inline state copy (`app/src/client/components/ui/copy.ts`, `app/src/client/components/page/PageStates.tsx`):

| State | Copy |
| - | - |
| A read that failed, beside the thing that failed | "Couldn't load this. Nothing was changed.", with "Try again" |
| A resource that answers 404 (10.2) | "Not found. This may have been deleted, or it may never have been yours to see." |
| A response carrying truncated (section 6's cap rule) | "This list is long, so only the first part is shown. Narrow it down to see the rest." |
| A write refused by the storage quota (10.3) | "Out of space. Delete some files in Settings, or remove attachments you no longer need." |

The 404 sentence never distinguishes deleted from never-visible, which is 10.2's
non-disclosure rule said aloud. The inline failed-read line is the page's own words and
never the global toast's: a page saying "Nothing was saved" where nothing was being saved
tells a person something false about their own data.
