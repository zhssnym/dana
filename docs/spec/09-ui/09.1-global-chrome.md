### 9.1 Global chrome

- Sidebar: a navigation landmark and the application's only side chrome. Header carrying
  the app name (links to /app), the notifications bell (9.12), and the global search
  control (Ctrl+K or /). Then the page block, in fixed order: Desk, Today, Scheduled,
  Assignments, Important, All, plus any unpinned view the pin list of 9.7 has pinned
  (Done, Abandoned, Anytime, and the viewer's saved views), each present only when pinned
  and each a link; then a "More" row opening onto whatever native pages the pin list has
  not pinned, each a link, its open state the browser's (amended at the polish round of
  2026-08-30: Done, Anytime and Abandoned ship unpinned and the search overlay was their
  only door). Then the Library block; then the footer. - Library: a collapsible block
  headed "Library", holding the viewer's shelves and folders as one ordered list. A shelf
  is a collapsible group of folder rows headed by its name; folders on no shelf follow.
  Each folder row carries the folder's mark (its icon in its colour, 4.3), its name, and
  its pending count. The order is the viewer's own, seeded alphabetically and rearranged
  by dragging: rows reorder, move onto and off shelves, and two folders held together
  create a shelf; shelf headings drag as rows too. A group being closed is never sprung
  open by a drag hovering it; the drop reads "Into {shelf}". The block's heading carries a
  "New" control, drawn as a "+" and resting visible rather than revealed on hover, which
  begins New folder, New shelf or New task; it is the one door to the three acts of
  making, and it survives the icon rail where the heading itself does not (added at the
  polish round of 2026-08-30, on the owner's own ruling that emptied the footer: "just put
  them into a '+' icon just like Imran said, somewhere around easy to find in the
  sidebar". The place is the prototype's, which draws a heading control there; that it
  rests visible is this round's, on the same reading 9.11 states as "a row whose only
  controls appear on hover is a row with nothing to press", a touchscreen having no hover
  to give.) Collapsed to the icon rail the Library draws only the folder the viewer is
  standing in and the folders the tab strip holds open, keeping the order above and
  drawing no shelf headings and no counts; the Library is rearranged at its own width and
  not from the rail. (Amended 2026-08-30, on the owner's instruction: the rail drew one
  unlabelled glyph per folder the account has, which is a wall rather than a list.
  AC-182's third case asserts the older reading, that the rail reaches every folder, and
  is escalated with this amendment rather than being satisfied by it.) - Footer: the
  avatar menu, and nothing else. The menu ("Account") carries Settings, People, Trash,
  Import (a workflow file), Help and Sign out, in that order: the places this account
  owns, then the act and the sheet that are about the application rather than about your
  work, then the door behind a separator. (Amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30, the
  owner's own ruling, and it supersedes sidebar F3, F8 and F10 of the same day rather than
  sitting beside them. His words: "leave more real estate to the Library in the sidebar,
  and move Import and People and Settings and Trash to when you click on the profile! … my
  goal is that there is more space for the Library so people can see a big picture of
  what's going on in their app." What this replaces read: "one primary 'New folder'
  action, which opens the folder it makes; then a row of two acts of making — 'New shelf'
  and 'Import' (a workflow file) — each keeping its label at every width and both named by
  tooltip in every state. Then Trash, People and Settings as links; then the avatar menu
  ('Account': Help, Sign out)." Three consequences and none is optional. The three acts of
  making are the Library's "New" control, above. DES-216's narrowing asymmetry, and the
  whole question of which label survives a drag, is spent with the row it described: a
  menu has no width to run out of. And the three places are menu items where they were
  links, which is a role this change spends deliberately — the roles table's "the page
  entries … are links" never reached them, these three never having been in the page
  block, and the gesture a link's middle click gave is answered by the tab strip's "+",
  which opens any place as a tab.) Below 768px the sidebar is a drawer, opened by a "Menu"
  control on a phone bar above the tab strip that also carries the mark, "Search" and the
  bell; the drawer closes on navigation, on its backdrop and on Escape. Below that width
  the task surface of 9.3 takes the whole width in either mode and draws no resize handle,
  there being nothing for a hand to drag against. The Library is the only place a shelf or
  folder is made from besides the shelf's own "+" and the shelf page; the word for a group
  of folders is "shelf" in every dialog, menu, toast and confirm, and a shelf may be made
  empty. - Tab strip: a row of tabs above the window bar, session-scoped working state,
  never stored server-side. Every tab is the same tab: one anatomy (mark, title, close
  control), equal treatment, closable, and reorderable by drag. The place the viewer
  stands on is the active tab. "Open in a new tab" on a folder, shelf, or task appends a
  tab and goes to it; ordinary navigation retargets the active tab, as an address bar
  does. Closing the active tab lands on the neighbouring place; closing the last tab lands
  on Desk. A "+" beside the last tab opens any shelf or folder as a tab, or begins New
  folder, New shelf, New task, or Import. Every tab is the same width within one floor and
  one ceiling, and the tabs sit flush against one another rather than as separated pills:
  one to three are wide and even, a filling strip narrows them toward the floor rather
  than pushing the last one out, and past the floor the row scrolls. (Amended at the
  polish round of 2026-08-30, tabstrip F2, F3, F4, F9, F10 and F11: navigation to a place
  a tab already holds makes that tab active rather than retargeting another, and the "+"
  goes to that tab rather than opening a second; the active tab is drawn lifted against a
  tinted strip, the strip being ground and the tab a card; a focused tab closes on Delete
  or Backspace and any tab on a middle click, Ctrl+W being the browser's; and the sole
  remaining tab draws no close control, closing it having only ever meant going to Desk.
  Amended again at that round, tabstrip F13: the floor, the ceiling and the flush row are
  `prototype-v2`'s own, which the port had dropped for tabs sized to their own words with
  a gutter between them, and sharing the width between the two is this round's, the
  prototype drawing every tab at its floor however much room the strip had; and "New task"
  joins the acts the "+" begins, opening a plain task rather than one of 9.3's entry
  points, the strip belonging to the window and not to the page under it. New folder and
  New shelf are named there exactly as the footer names them, without an ellipsis, on this
  section's own rule for what an ellipsis means.) - Window bar: a thin strip under the
  tabs belonging to the window, not the page. Its left end holds the active filter's
  removable chips; its right end holds the active tab's controls — the view strip, the
  filter control, the Activity line ("{verb} {ago}", opening the recent events of the
  thing shown), and the object's overflow menu. The page's title is not repeated in it;
  the tab already says it. A page adds its own controls to that end where the control
  belongs to the window rather than to the page's body: Assignments puts its
  whose-assignments scope control there (9.5), and the folder page its member faces and
  its Share control, both drawn as quiet chrome rather than as filled blocks. (Amended at
  the polish round of 2026-08-30, folderpage F10: the folder's faces and its Share button
  stood beside the folder's name, which is the composition DES-139 replaced everywhere
  else, and which put two tiers of control within forty pixels of each other and scrolled
  the page's own chrome away with its body.) - The view strip: one control per place-page
  offering the views the page can honestly draw — list always; board wherever the page has
  a grouping (a board column captures with its own value, and dropping a card in a column
  writes that value); calendar only where having a date is what puts a task on the page,
  which is Scheduled. The chosen view is one client-side preference across pages; a page
  not offering the remembered view falls back to list without forgetting it. `?mode=`
  names a view in the URL. The strip is icons rather than written labels, and it rests
  open: every view the page offers is drawn at once, one segmented control (amended at the
  polish round of 2026-08-30; it rested collapsed until then, and a hover was the only way
  to learn that a page had a board or a calendar at all, which a touchscreen cannot make).
  The options it does not draw stay present and reachable, at no width rather than
  removed, so the keyboard and a screen reader always meet the whole group; only the
  current option holds the tab stop. - The filter: one control with the same meaning on
  every place-page — due presence and ranges, assignee (multi-value, "Unassigned"
  included), important, repeating, created-by, show-abandoned, group-by, sort, and "Save
  as view". Active state renders as removable chips in the window bar, never as a count
  alone. A filter belongs to the page and to the session: kept on return to that page,
  never carried to another. Grouping replaces the page's own arrangement rather than
  nesting inside it. - Capture: each page has one always-visible capture point. Quick-add
  rows sit where a page has a natural landing place (a section, a group, a list foot); in
  board view every column captures and the floating button is absent; the floating "Add
  task" button appears only on the pages with no capture affordance of their own — Today,
  Important, and Assignments — and never on Done or Abandoned. Today carries the quick-add
  row as well, at its list foot, since the polish round of 2026-08-30: every other list
  page has one and Today asking for its own separate word for the same job was the round's
  taskrow F12; the floating button stays because it is where the full form is reached from
  a page whose rows live elsewhere. Every capture creates the task with the entry defaults
  of 9.3 for where it stands, so a task made on a page appears on that page; where the
  list it was typed into carries a hand order (9.6), the new task lands at its foot, in
  typing order (polish round, 2026-08-30). - Undo: one bar for the whole window,
  bottom-left, naming the act it can take back ("Deleted "{title}""), for five seconds,
  with Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z as its shortcut; no redo. Acts register their own inverse. The
  bar's sentence and 4.13's event are separately specified: the event is written
  server-side inside the mutation's transaction and the bar's wording is this section's,
  so neither is the source of the other (RFC 0005 change control item 57, B6, confirming
  item 44). Undo issues the inverse mutation — no write is ever held back waiting for the
  window to close. - The mark: Dana's mark is drawn inline as SVG rather than fetched as
  an image, everywhere it appears (the sidebar header, the tab strip, the landing and
  invite pages, the identity page). Its rest state is the whole logo, so a mark that never
  animates is simply the logo; on hover of its enclosing link, button or menu item a pen
  mask uncovers it along the ribbon once and finishes rather than snapping back; each
  instance's mask id is unique; `prefers-reduced-motion` turns the motion off. The icon
  family is likewise animated: a glyph draws, wiggles or pops on the hover of its
  enclosing control, and a region may switch the motion off wholesale (the sidebar does,
  for its rows). (Folded from `prototype-v2` at RFC 0005 change control item 57, D: ledger
  L-117, L-141 to L-145.) - Object acts: every kind of thing (task, folder, shelf, the
  surface) declares its acts once and every overflow menu draws them through one
  component, so where a person stands never decides what they may do to a thing. The acts
  sit in five groups in a fixed order — reach it, change it, copy it, stop it, destroy it
  — the destructive group last and behind a separator; stopping (Abandon, "I am not doing
  this") and destroying (Delete, "this should not exist") are two groups and never one. An
  act nothing supplies a handler for is not drawn, so a surface cannot offer what it
  cannot do; a surface may add rendered extra items of its own above the contract's acts
  and only rendered ones. A task's open act carries the full-screen glyph. (Item 57, D:
  L-002 to L-006, L-008, L-014.) - The view strip's disabled option, where a page has one,
  names why in its tooltip instead of its name. The window bar is filled by a page
  portalling into it, so a page keeps its own state while drawing into shell chrome. A
  side panel takes room from the page rather than covering it; its width is one number in
  a store the shell reads, shared by every panel, so two cannot overlap. Menus, lists and
  pickers share one row primitive. (Item 57, D: L-034, L-136, L-138, L-139, L-214.) -
  Library drag: the sidebar's sorting is pointer-driven rather than HTML5 drag-and-drop,
  so it works on touch and auto-scrolls; the carried row follows the pointer and the rows
  it passes slide out of its way by its own height, nothing being outlined; the same list
  sorts by keyboard, the handle a real button, arrows moving the item and focus following.
  The proposal pill of a hold rides the carried row rather than the row beneath, and the
  hold is drawn as a closing ring that starts from empty every time. (Item 57, D: L-090,
  L-091, L-093, L-127, L-128.) - Offline banner (global, persistent while offline):
  "You're offline. Dana needs a connection; changes can't be saved right now." Writes
  attempted offline fail with the toast "Couldn't save. You're offline." - Global error
  toast for failed requests: "Something went wrong. Nothing was saved. Try again." 409
  conflicts show the state machine copy of 5.1 verbatim; a stale page-block write (4.17)
  uses the same treatment.

The two retired v1 paths redirect rather than 404, because bookmarks exist: /app/planned
to /app/scheduled, and /app/tasks to /app. A hidden (unpinned) page still renders at its

inaccessible or absent id answers with the 404 copy of this section under 10.2.

Addressing. Every control this section names is reachable by its accessible role and by
its normative name; the copy above is what a screen reader announces and what a test
addresses. Roles, carried and amended:

| Element | Role |
| - | - |
| The sidebar | A navigation landmark |
| The page entries, and each folder and shelf row | Links |
| A task row | A list item, or a row |
| A tab in the strip | A tab, current when active |
| The task surface | A dialog when opened as one; a page when opened as a tab or by its URL |
| A toggle switch | A switch |
| A calendar day | A grid cell within a grid |
| A board column | A named region; its cards, list items or rows |
| A view option in the strip | A radio within a radiogroup, checked when current |

An ellipsis in a label is normative and means one thing: a further step follows before
anything happens. The five acts that carry one are "Move to folder…", "Delete task…",
"Delete folder…", "Leave folder…" and "Delete shelf…", each opening a confirmation or a
picker; an act that takes effect on the press carries none.

Regions. Every section a page's structure names is a named region; the name is the heading
where this section gives one and the datum the section is generated from where it does
not. Per page: the sidebar, one region per shelf named by it; Today, "Carried over",
"Today", "Done today (N)"; Scheduled's list, one per ladder rung named as its heading
reads; Assignments, one per person named by them, "Unassigned" last; All, its two halves'
regions as their home pages define them; Done, one per day group; a folder page, one per
user section named by it, plus "Done" and "Sharing panel"; a board, one region per column
named by its value; the tab strip's tabs, each addressed by its title. A collapsible
section remains a named region plus a control carrying that name, whose expanded state is
what open and collapsed mean.

Inline state copy, normative and unchanged from v1:

| State | Copy |
| - | - |
| A read that failed, beside the thing that failed | "Couldn't load this. Nothing was changed.", with "Try again" |
| A resource that answers 404 (10.2) | "Not found. This may have been deleted, or it may never have been yours to see." |
| A response carrying truncated (section 6's cap rule) | "This list is long, so only the first part is shown. Narrow it down to see the rest." |
| A write refused by the storage quota (10.3) | "Out of space. Delete some files in Settings, or remove attachments you no longer need." |

The inline failed-read line is the page's own words and never the global toast's; the two
answer different questions, and a page showing "Nothing was saved" where nothing was being
saved tells a person something false about their own data.
