### 9.13 First run and the starter workflows

A person signing in for the first time arrives at Desk (9.1) holding nothing. That empty
Desk is the one screen every account sees and no account keeps, and it is where the
product either teaches itself or does not.

Beneath Desk's own empty state, a fresh account is offered the starter workflows: bundled
workflow files (7.16) that import with one press, each a worked example of a real piece of
work rather than a tour of the interface. The purpose is navigation. A person who imports
one has a folder in their Library, a page with sections, and tasks reaching the views of
9.4 and 9.5, which teaches where things live by putting something in each place rather
than by describing it.

**Fresh** means the account holds no folder but Desk and no task anywhere. Both facts are
already in what the client read to draw the page, so the offer needs no endpoint of its
own. It stops being drawn the moment either is false, which includes the moment an import
lands, and it does not return: a person who imports one or dismisses it has left first
run, and asking again would be the product failing to notice it had already been answered.

**Each starter is an ordinary import.** POST /api/v1/workflows/import (7.16) takes the
file, through the same endpoint and the same validation a person's own exported workflow
goes through. Nothing is seeded, nothing is special-cased in the server, and no row is
created that a person could not have created themselves. This is why the feature costs no
backend, and it is also why it cannot drift from the import: a starter that stopped
importing would fail the same way any workflow file would.

**What a starter may contain is what a workflow file may contain**: a folder name, task
titles, and page blocks. It follows that a starter carries no comment and no reminder, and
this section does not enforce that, because 7.16's schema has no key for either and a
constraint the format makes impossible needs no rule. What is recorded here is why it is
right that the format forbids them, so that a later widening of the format does not take
these two with it by accident.

A comment would have to be attributed to somebody. Every comment in Dana is a thing a
person said (4.15), and a fabricated one on a fresh account would be the product inventing
a colleague, which is a lie about the one subject a collaboration tool cannot afford to
lie about. A reminder would send email about a plan the person did not make, at an hour
they did not choose, most probably after they had forgotten the starter was there; the
person likeliest to receive it is the one who never came back, and a starter that mails a
person who has left is the worst version of this feature.

**Every starter says in its own page that it can be deleted**, as the first paragraph
block of its folder, because the offer's own copy is gone the moment it is answered and
the page is what the person still has.

Copy, normative:

| Element | Copy |
| - | - |
| The offer's heading | "Start with an example" |
| The offer's body | "Import a worked example to see how Dana holds a real piece of work. Nothing here is fixed: change anything, or delete it all." |
| Dismiss | "No thanks" |
| The first paragraph block of every starter folder | "This is an example, and it is yours now. Change anything you like, or delete the whole folder from its menu when you are done with it." |

The starter files themselves are the design record's, on the 9.2 ruling of the same date:
this section fixes that they exist, what they may hold, and what they must say, and does
not transcribe them. What they must be is worked examples of problems a person actually
has, each one solved the way Dana solves it, because a starter that demonstrates the
interface rather than the work teaches a person how to click and not why to return.

**The sample workspace, and where the offer stands beside it (RFC 0005 change control item
60, 2026-08-30).** A brand-new account no longer arrives holding nothing: 7.1 writes the
sample workspace of 7.20 in the provisioning transaction, so the first Desk a person sees
is full, and the offer above is not drawn, its freshness test being false while the
sample's folders stand. Above Desk and Today, while the sample is present, stands a strip:
one sentence, "This is a sample workspace to show you around.", and two controls, "Remove
sample data" and "Keep it". Remove confirms once, "Remove the sample workspace? Everything
it added goes; anything you made yourself stays.", then removes through DELETE /sample and
offers the undo bar reading "Sample workspace removed", whose inverse is POST /sample. A
sample folder removal left standing because it is shared is said in a toast. Keep it
dismisses the strip on this device only; 9.7's row is the way back. Removal empties the
account, so the offer above is drawn again exactly when it should be: after the person has
chosen to start from nothing.
