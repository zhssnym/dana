### 9.6 Folder page

The folder page is a page in the full sense: a cover it can be given; its mark (the
identity control — pressing it opens the icon-and-colour picker, writing 4.3's shared
identity); its name (inline rename, owner only; members read a heading); its editable
description; and its body — one borderless compose-first canvas of Sections, prose, images,
and real task rows (4.17), with zero-height insertion seams between every item and the Done
section collapsed at the foot
(named "Completed" until the polish round of 2026-08-30: the finished pile was Done in two
places and Completed in three, which reads as two states). Its board groups by section,
and the column holding the tasks under none is named "No section" where the page has
sections and "Tasks" where it has none (amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30: "No
section" on a page with no sections read as something missing); while the board is shown
the Done section is not drawn beneath it, the Done column being the one place a finished
task stands. Ordering within sections and the block arrangement are the folder's, shared
by every member; the filter of 9.1 narrows the page without touching its arrangement. The
folder's conversation has two homes — a closed section at the page's foot, or the side
panel — switched from the surface itself; a message is told apart by tone, never hue;
likes are the whole reaction set.

Clicking a seam writes directly there; its `+`, or `/` on an empty prose line, offers exactly
Task, Section, and Image. Enter splits prose, Shift+Enter is a soft break, and Ctrl/Command +
Enter converts persisted prose into a real task in place. A Section is a semantic boundary:
moving a task across it changes the task's Section, and collapsing it hides every item whose
stored Section names it. The `+` on a collapsed Section stands after its complete hidden span
and inserts into `No section`, never into the collapsed Section. YouTube uses the
privacy-enhanced player and Vimeo uses its player; direct mp4/webm/ogg URLs remain ordinary
external links. Automatic link/product thumbnails, multi-select drag, workflow
round-tripping, collaborative editing and a universal editor framework are outside #184.

Who may do what, the role matrix (RFC 0005, recording DES-225):

| Act | Owner | Member |
| - | - | - |
| Rename, Delete | yes | no — the name is the folder's address, and delete ends it for everybody |
| Leave | only via ownership transfer (below) | yes |
| Tasks: create, edit, complete, abandon, delete-to-Trash | yes | yes |
| The page: blocks, cover, icon, colour, description | yes | yes — the event log says who |
| History, Copy link, Duplicate, Save as workflow | yes | yes |
| Share, remove a member | per the 5.4 matrix, unchanged | per 5.4 |
| Shelf placement, sidebar arrangement, label colour | personal, always | personal, always |

A surface offers exactly the acts the viewer's role permits — Leave where Delete would be,
never both — and the server refuses the rest regardless (10.2). Deleting moves the folder
to Trash for every member at once, with the undo bar and no confirmation; members are
notified (4.14). Leaving confirms — the way back is an invitation, not a Trash row —
writes no Trash entry, offers no undo, and records "Left": "Leave Work? You lose access to
it, and anything in it assigned to you becomes unassigned." An owner with other members
leaves only by transferring ownership first (7.16), choosing a successor, defaulting to
the longest-standing member per 8.9's order; a sole owner is offered Delete instead, 5.3's
rule unchanged. Remove-a-member keeps its v1 confirmation verbatim. Each confirmation
renders as one sentence, restyled never reworded.

Duplicate takes the structure and never the grants or the progress: tasks, sections,
paragraphs, and section assignments copy; members, sharing, the conversation, assignees,
and completion do not; spent due dates re-anchor on today and future ones keep, and the
bar says which happened (L-055). The copy is the duplicator's own private folder, and its
tasks are assigned to whoever made the copy, never left empty, so the copy appears in the
duplicator's Assignments (item 44's B10, checked against `domain/acts.ts` at item 51(h)
and pinned by AC-277; recorded here at item 57). The History sheet groups by day and names
the actor on every line, and its lines and the row's popover are one sentence drawn
through one formatter (item 57, D: L-199, L-200).

The sharing panel carries v1's spec unchanged: member list with joined dates and remove
controls per 5.4, roster picker, email invite, pending list with Cancel and Resend
("Resent recently" inside 24 hours). It is opened from the Share control in the window bar
of 9.1, beside the folder's member faces, rather than from the page's head (polish round,
2026-08-30, folderpage F10); the control is withheld on the default folder, which INV-2
makes single-member, and the faces are drawn only where there is more than one.

The "Label and color" dialog is retired: the folder's icon and colour are the shared
identity above, set from the mark; a member's personal shelf placement is "Move to shelf"
on the row; and 4.4's per-member color column is no longer written by any v2 surface
(existing values are simply unused — the column is frozen and stays).

**Annotation, 2026-09-04, issue #118: what the cover picker offers, and on which pages.
Stated here once and pointed at from 9.10 rather than restated there.** The picker has four
tabs in the design and draws three: **Gallery** (the gradients and the ribbon marks),
**Upload** (a picture of the person's own, stored and therefore surviving a reload) and
**Photos** (an openly licensed photograph, which the server fetches and stores so that the
cover is a file this origin serves). **Link is drawn on no page**: 10.8's `img-src` admits
no external host, so a pasted URL would render as the flat fallback, and the honest fix is a
server-side fetch with an SSRF surface of its own; issue #115 holds it, and a tier a surface
cannot honestly draw is a tier it does not draw rather than a disabled one (DES-144).

**The same three are offered on all three kinds of page** — this one, a shelf's (9.10) and
one of 4.20's built-in places — with the same wording, the same reposition and the same
words when a write is refused, which is Imran's ruling on #118. Until this date a shelf
drew no cover control at all and a built-in place drew the gallery alone, because 7.15 could
not make a picture that was not some folder's; issues #20 and #100 removed that limit.
**Where the picture is kept still differs and stays each section's own**: 7.4's three columns
for a folder, 7.16's for a shelf, 7.19's row for a place — so a folder's cover is one look
for every member, a shelf's is private, and a place's is per viewer. AC-406 asserts the two
new pages in a browser and AC-342 the folder page's own.
