### 9.6 Folder page

The folder page (`app/src/client/pages/FolderPage.tsx`) is a page in the full sense: a
cover, then a header — its mark (pressing it opens the icon-and-colour picker, writing 4.3's
shared identity) and its name (click to rename), with "Share" and one "…" at the right. There is no progress count ("N of M done"): nobody
measures a page by it, and a task's own subtask count stays on its row. There is no description line: the
Purpose tab holds a page's why, and a description already stored stays in the data, undrawn.
Then a row of plain text tabs (the project's tabs, 4.3) and the tab's body. The header is the
project's on every tab.

Every page, at any depth, wears this same head; the page draws no breadcrumb of its own. A
page's path is the window bar's breadcrumb (9.1), which names only ancestors the viewer is a
member of (the server cuts the walk at the first one they are not). A page moves by dragging
its sidebar row, into another page or back to the top level; the "…" has no move item.

**A new project** (New project in the Library's "+" and right-click menu, beside Import
project) has no kind until someone picks one: its whole background offers **The usual
project** first (Purpose, Work, Results and Discussion), then Doc, Work and Discussion on
their own, each with one line saying what it is for, and it stays until one is picked. A Doc
page is writing that may hold tasks and pages; a Work page is tasks and the words around them;
a Discussion page is the project's threads. **With one tab the tab itself is hidden**, so a quick note
still reads as a page. Its "+" sits in the header beside Share, except on Work, where it stays on
the hairline beside the Filter and View controls. The tab row is in stored order; tabs are reordered by dragging them on
the row — a mouse press that moves, or a long press on touch, lifts the tab, which follows the
pointer at reduced opacity while a thin accent line marks where it will land; Esc cancels;
Alt+←/→ on a focused tab moves it one place — written through `PATCH /folders/:id/location`.
A quiet "+" after the last tab offers Doc and Work, and Discussion while the project has none.
Right-clicking a tab offers Rename (in place) and Delete; the project's own tab has neither,
its name being the project's. "Discuss" on a task in a project without a Discussion adds that
tab, then opens the thread. A sub-page has no tabs; it is drawn by its own kind under the
project's header. A finished task stays where it was
placed, drawn in the thread with its state; there is no separate Done section, no "Completed
Task" line. Ordering within sections and the block
arrangement are the folder's, shared by every member. Desk, the default folder, is drawn by
this page like any other folder. A folder page is where finished and dropped tasks are
seen, in place; no page gathers them across folders.

**Work is one document with Thread, Freeform, Board and Calendar views.** Thread is the initial
default; a viewer may choose a preferred default in Settings and switch views on a Work
page without changing its stored order, Sections or Tasks. Thread draws the document with a left rail
(`app/src/client/components/page/canvas/thread/`); Freeform draws it without the rail. Each block keeps
its editing and renders as itself, in full; the rail adds a small square badge per block, threaded
on one line: a heading is a numbered stop (1, 2, 3 in section order), a paragraph a faint mark,
a page link a page mark. A heading owns the blocks under it, and a sub-heading's blocks stay on its
parent's rail. A task row is its state mark (To do, Done, or Dropped, which is struck and never
drawn as done), its title, and on the right its day when it has one and, on a page with
more than one member, the assignee's face; a finished task shows the day it was done and names nobody. A task whose blocker is still open, or whose start date is after today, is waiting,
with an amber pause for its mark: one with an open blocker reads "waits on" and the blocker's
title, and one waiting only on its start date shows its day. The line is solid down to the first open task in document order and dashed after it, in
every later section too, so a fully solid thread is a finished project; from the first
finished task to that first open task it is drawn in the done colour, the finished work as
one stretch, and a tick by the viewer grows it to the next open task. Nothing on the thread
counts. **The next task** is the first task in document order
that is open, not waiting and, on a page with more than one member, assigned to the viewer or to no one: its ring and title carry the accent, the only
accent on the thread, and there is at most one. Headings on the thread never fold. Five or
more finished tasks before a section's first open one show only their last three behind an
"Earlier work" break, drawn and never stored: opening it lasts the session, and a task ticked
while the page is open stays where it is until the next load. The last line is always an empty task line, "Add a task…":
typing makes a task, `/` opens the block menu and `# ` makes a heading; Enter at the end of
any line makes another line of its kind. On a phone that line stays pinned above the
keyboard and files a new task into the section in view. When the page has tasks and none is
open, one line under it, "Write what came of it", opens Results. Clicking a task opens its page
(9.3) and clicking its mark ticks it; Back returns to the same place in the thread. An old
`?issue=<id>` address lands on `/app/tasks/<id>`.

In Freeform Work, a task row shows a small expand control on hover or focus. Expanding it
shows the same Task properties, notes, subtasks and history directly below the row, within
the document's hierarchy. The Task stays the same record and edits there use its usual
write path; collapsing the row leaves the document in place. Other Work views do not
expand Task rows.

Work has compact **Filter** and **View** icon controls on the tab hairline, separated from the tabs
and "+". Filter narrows Task placements by status, assignee,
date or a chosen day, importance, repetition and Label; axes combine, and active axes appear as removable
chips with Clear all. Filtering is only a view of the same Work: it never moves, duplicates
or reorders a Task, changes its Section, hides an authored heading or prose block, advances
the progress rail, or chooses a different canonical Next Task. Completed and dropped Tasks
remain in their authored positions and may be shown or hidden by the filter. While a filter
is active, writing and arrangement are paused so invisible Task placements cannot be moved.
With no active filter the chips are absent.

Board is a projection of the same Tasks. Its compact Group by menu sits beside the Filter and
View controls on the tab hairline, showing the selected Section, Assignee or Date choice.
With Section grouping, root Sections are columns,
H2 and H3 Sections stay nested within their root, and Tasks appear under their exact Section;
loose Tasks appear under No section. Presentation headings make no Board group. Assignee and
Date grouping rearrange those same Tasks without changing their Work location, and show the
Section path where useful. A drag into a Section, assignee or date group updates that Task's
canonical property. The Work filter applies to Board as it does to Thread and Freeform.

Calendar is the same treatment as Upcoming (9.5), scoped to this Work's Tasks and
sharing its Task filter. Month, Week, Day, navigation, zoom, repeating occurrences
and Task status all use that calendar. Clicking an empty day creates a Task here
with that Due Date and no Start Date. A Start+Due Task draws one continuous opaque range
bar over the top of its day cells; dragging its body moves both dates and its ends edit Start
and Due. A Due-only Task gains Start by dragging its left edge backward, and a
Start-only Task gains Due by dragging its right edge forward. The view changes
no Section, Work order or Task identity, and may be the default Work view.


The page's "…" draws a list from `app/src/client/lib/object-acts.ts`. On a project and its
tabs it is the project's: History, Copy link (once), Duplicate, Export project, then Leave
project… or Delete project as the viewer's role permits. On a sub-page it is the page's:
History, Copy link, Duplicate, then Leave project… or Delete page. There is no Rename (the
title renames in place), no Add a page inside (the `/` menu's Page makes a sub-page) and no
Import as sub-page… (the Library's Import project brings a file in; `POST /folders/:id/import`
stays). Copy link copies silently, and Duplicate says so by landing on the copy. **Export
project** downloads the project as a Dana file (`<name>.dana.json`) and says in one line what
it leaves out (dates, done tasks, sub-pages, the discussion). **Transfer ownership…** is in
Share, which is about who else gets the page.
Tabs are managed on the tab row alone. Deleting a tab or a page is never confirmed: it goes to
Trash with Undo, and a deleted page lands on the page it sat in.

Clicking a task placement on the canvas (or Enter on it) opens the task as every list row
does. A placement has one menu, the gutter's ⋮⋮, whose Delete deletes the task (with the
row's confirmation and undo); the row's own "More actions" is not drawn on the canvas. A
small + beside Work's block handle inserts a blank block immediately before that block,
including a Task at the top of the page, and puts the caret there. On touch, a long press on
a block offers "Insert block above"; Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Enter does the same from the keyboard.
The new block stays in the same Section as the block that followed it.

A failed save shows one quiet line, "Not saved — Retry", the line the Task and Shelf writers
show; a failure Retry cannot re-send (a conflict, or the page changed elsewhere) offers
"Load latest", which keeps the draft in the tab and offers it back, and no other error does.
A gesture the canvas refuses says why in that same line, never a second one, and the line
takes no height when it has nothing to say. Unsaved writing left by an earlier session is
offered back above the writing in the same words on every writer: "Unsaved writing from
before is kept." with Download writing, Restore (where it can be put back) and Discard.

The project's conversation has one home: its Discussion tab, a list of the threads of every
page of the project, whose General discussions (which cannot be moved to Trash) are the pages'
own. A link to the conversation (`?conversation=open`) lands there, and an old tab address
(`?facet=<id>`) opens `/app/folders/<id>`, since a tab keeps its facet's id. The tab's name is heavier while any open thread has a post the reader has not read; nothing else marks it. The tab is two layers. The list is one bordered box with a hairline between rows, like an issues list, most recent activity first (no groups, no counts, no column headers; an old `?threads=` address is ignored). Its first row is a tinted head: on the left two filters, "Open" and "Resolved", the chosen one in ink and the other muted, Open by default and kept only while the page is open; on the right, for someone who may start a thread, a small primary "New thread" button, which puts a title line at the top of the list (Enter creates, Escape cancels). The list shows only the threads matching the filter, or one quiet line, "No open threads" or "No resolved threads". Each thread is one compact line with one leading mark before its title: a thread about a task shows the task's small state mark, linking to the task; any other thread an outline speech bubble while open and a filled check circle once resolved, and pressing it (or Space on the row) resolves the thread without a decision or reopens it, with Undo. A resolved thread is the same row, muted. The title follows the mark, at medium weight and cut short with an ellipsis before the meta, heavier when someone else has posted since the reader last read it (kept by the server per person, 4.34, so every device agrees); on the right of the same line, small and muted: the last poster and when ("Lucía Ortega · 4 days ago", "You" for the reader's own), or "Resolved · Sep 18" once resolved; no post preview, no face, and no post is counted. The list refreshes itself about every twenty seconds. An open thread is its own page: the page's head and tabs step aside, the top bar's breadcrumb names the thread and its page crumb leads back to the list, and the thread opens at the first post from someone else since the last read, with one "New" hairline above it for that visit, or else at its end, where on a desktop the cursor waits in the composer. Escape (from anywhere but a field holding writing or an open menu) goes back to the list, with focus on that thread's row. Its head reads like a letter's: the 28px title on the left, a rule under the head, and, on the right, "Turn into a task" (an open thread other than the General discussion and not already about a task, for someone who may edit it and create tasks there), "Resolve" (an outlined button with a check) while it is open, and a "…" menu (Rename, Move to Trash). A thread about a task shows one plain row under its title linking to the task: its state mark, the task's live title and its state ("To do", "Done" or "Dropped"). The project's Discussion is the only place a page's people talk, a task's talk included: "Discuss" on a task (9.3) opens that task's one thread here, made on first use and titled as the task. "Turn into a task" is an open thread's main act: it creates a task titled as the thread at the end of the page's Work, and links the thread to it (`task_id`, 7.28); the thread stays open as the task's conversation, and the task page links back to it. Deleting the task later leaves the thread standing, without the link. The thread fills the window: its posts read from the top, under the title, the history scrolls in its own region, and the composer follows the last post, staying at the foot of the window once the history fills it; a grouped post shows its time in the gutter on hover. Move to Trash asks nothing: the list returns at once with an Undo. Then its posts oldest first, re-read every few seconds while the thread is open (a reader at the end stays at the end as posts arrive; one scrolled up gets a quiet "New posts" line above the composer instead), each post that answers nothing followed by its replies as one indented, quieter run (replying to a reply joins its thread's one level), and grouped as a chat groups them: a post by the same person within five minutes of the one before it in the same run, on the same day, draws only its text under the first one's small face, name and time. The time beside a name is the clock for today and the day and clock before that ("Yesterday 14:05", "24 Sep 14:05"); there are no day dividers. Deleting a post keeps its replies (they become plain posts). A resolved thread has no composer: under its title one short block says "Resolved <date> by <name>", then its decision if one was given, with "Reopen" beside it. A post is written in the canvas's editor (headings, lists, checklists, quotes, code, images), less the pages-only blocks; its empty box names the one or two others who posted, latest first ("Reply to Sam and Lucía…"), else says "Write a reply…" ("Write the first post…" in an empty thread); Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line (in a list, a code block, an open menu or on a touch screen Enter stays the editor's, and Ctrl/Cmd+Enter or Send posts). The composer is one bordered box: the writing, then a row with an attach button, an "@" button that opens the mention menu, and "Send", grey until there is something to send; a picture or file in a post shows its name and size. A sent post leaves the box at once and shows in the thread at once, muted until the server has it, so whatever is typed meanwhile stays and a second Enter sends a second post after the first. The members of a page never share a face's colour there, and a mention reads as plain "@Name" on the line, tinted, with no face. "Resolve" puts an optional "What was decided?" line where the composer was, in the composer's own box with "Resolve" where Send was; likes are the whole reaction set. A liked post carries one small thumbs-up chip under its text naming who liked it ("You", "Lucía and You"), never a count, and pressing it likes or unlikes. Like, Reply and "…" (Edit, Delete) show on hover in a small bar inside the post's own row, never over the post above; Reply names the post over the composer.

The body is a BlockNote editor; `app/src/client/components/page/canvas/` is the truth for
the surface, and `app/src/client/components/page/canvas/schema.ts` fixes the vocabulary (prose, Section headings, images,
real task rows of 4.17; bold and italic). Two things are contract rather than surface:

- **A Section is a semantic boundary**: it owns the items nested under it, and moving a task
  across one changes the task's Section. It is drawn as a heading on the thread's rail, and
  what it holds sits on that rail; dragging the heading carries the whole section. A heading that folds is a toggle
  heading, which is writing and owns no Task. "Heading n" and `#` make the same Section where it
  is typed: inside a Section of that level or deeper it steps out to its level and takes what
  followed it along, so nothing moves on screen; an empty line becomes the Section itself.
- **A Task is made in place from a block**: "Task" in the `/` menu or "Turn into task" on
  the block turns a line with prose straight into a Task titled by that prose, keeping the
  block's position; on an empty line the line itself becomes the task being titled (a
checkbox and "Task title"), created on Enter or on leaving it, and removed if left empty.
  Enter or Backspace on it while it is still empty ends it as on an empty to-do in Notion: the
  line stays, plain text, with the caret on it.
  `app/src/client/components/page/canvas/CanvasSurface.tsx` carries the routes. The `/`
  menu (`SLASH_MENUS` in `slash-items.tsx`) shows only what the
  page is for: on Work, Text, Heading 1–3 and Task; on Purpose and Results, Text, Heading 1–3,
  Checklist and Media. Each row is an icon, its name and the markdown shortcut for it. Every
  other block is made by its markdown shortcut or Turn into, and a sub-page from the `/` menu's Page. On Work there is no To-do list: Task is the checkbox. An empty Work page is its last line, "Add a
  task…" (an empty Purpose says "Write why this page exists", an empty Results
"Write what came of this", and a reader who cannot write sees neither); while writing, only the focused empty block shows a muted
  hint ("Press '/' for commands", "Heading", "List", or "What came of it" in a Result), gone at the first character, and saving says nothing — no "Saving…", no "Saved" — until a save fails.

Blocks are selected as in Notion (`app/src/client/components/page/canvas/CanvasMarquee.tsx`,
every canvas and the Task composer): dragging from empty space or the margins draws a faint
rectangle and the blocks it touches become selected, tinted; Shift-click extends, Esc in text
selects the block and Esc again clears, Cmd/Ctrl+A selects the block's text and then every
block. Selected blocks are deleted together, duplicated (Cmd/Ctrl+D), turned into another kind
(Cmd/Ctrl+/), nested (Tab/Shift-Tab) and dragged together by any one's handle. Arrow keys
move the selection a block, Shift+arrows extend it, and Enter goes back to writing at the
end of the block. The tint is one soft rounded shape a little larger than the run of
blocks. Tasks and Folder rows are never duplicated, and the guard still refuses a deletion
that holds a Task. Work ends in its last line, an empty Task line ("Add a task…"): a click
in the space below the thread lands there, and a second Enter on an empty line removes it.
In every other editor, a click in the space below the last block (the lower part of the
screen) writes on: into the last block if it is an empty line, otherwise into a new line
after it.

Video links embed only through YouTube's privacy-enhanced player and Vimeo's player (10.8's
`frame-src` admits nothing else); direct media URLs stay ordinary external links, and every
recognised URL is a semantic external link.

Who may do what is the Access ladder of 5.4, resolved by `app/src/shared/folder-actions.ts`,
which the routes themselves consult; the client reads it through
`app/src/client/lib/folder-authority.ts` and states no bar of its own. A surface offers
exactly the acts the viewer's Access permits — Leave where Delete would be, never both —
and the server refuses the rest regardless (10.2). Shelf placement, sidebar arrangement
and label colour are always personal.

- **Delete** moves the folder to Trash for every member at once, with the undo bar and no
  confirmation; members are notified (4.14). An owner with other members is asked nothing
  either (AC-283): handing the page on first is Transfer ownership in the "…", and the undo
  bar is the way back.
- **Leave** confirms, because the way back is an invitation rather than a Trash row; it
  writes no Trash entry, offers no undo, and records "Left". The confirmation says access is
  lost and anything assigned to the leaver becomes unassigned.
- **An owner with other members leaves only by transferring ownership first** (7.16),
  choosing a successor from the members who currently hold Full control, with no default
  among several and no order that means anything: joining first is no evidence anybody
  intended you to hold custody. A single candidate is already picked. Where no member holds
  Full control the picker says so and points at Share rather than presenting an empty list.
  The dialog says only the new owner can hand it back, and a toast names the new owner once
  it is done. A sole owner is offered Delete instead (5.3).
- Removing a member confirms (`app/src/client/pages/FolderSharingPanel.tsx`). Each confirmation is one
  sentence.

Duplicate takes the structure and never the grants or the progress: tasks, sections,
paragraphs and section assignments copy; members, sharing, the conversation, assignees and
completion do not; spent due dates re-anchor on today and future ones keep, and the bar
says which happened. The copy is the duplicator's own folder, and its tasks are
assigned to whoever made it, so the copy appears in their Assignments. It lands right
after the original: a sub-page's copy under the same parent (a sub-page like any other,
with its project's people) when the duplicator may add pages there, otherwise a
top-level page placed after the original in their Library and its collection. The History sheet
groups by day and names the actor on every line, through the same formatter as the row's
popover (`app/src/client/lib/history.ts`).

The sharing panel (`app/src/client/pages/FolderSharingPanel.tsx`) is the project's, from any
page of it: people belong to the project (step 8), so it lists the project's people and its
title names the project ("Share <project>"); Leave on a sub-page names the project too. It
has one "Name or email"
field, the Access the invite grants (the six presets, back to Can edit after each invite), and one
"Invite" button: the field suggests connections who are not yet members, and
Invite sends a page invite to the picked connection's address or the typed one, which the
server turns into an instant share when the address is already a connection (7.5). Below it
are the members, each with their Access ("Owner" for the owner): where the viewer may change
it, a menu of the six presets on the row, with "Remove from page" under them where 5.4 allows
removal (confirmed, never on the owner's row or the viewer's own, since leaving is Leave
page…). On the viewer's own row, marked "(you)", a change of Access confirms first, because
it drops them below Full control and they cannot raise it back. A viewer who may not share
sees the panel titled "Members of" the page rather than "Share", and the header button that opens it reads "Members". Then the pending invites, each with the Access it
grants and when it was sent, "Cancel", and a Resend shown only once 24 hours have passed since the last send. It opens from the "Share" button in the
folder's header; Share is withheld on the default folder, which INV-2 makes single-member.

A member's name in that list opens their details in the same panel
(`app/src/client/pages/MemberDetails.tsx`, #209); the panel's title becomes a trail (`Share <page> / <name>`) whose first crumb returns to the lists. The details show their Access,
a menu of the six presets where the viewer may change it (never on the owner's row, and sent
with the Access the panel showed, so a change made meanwhile is refused rather than
replaced), with "Remove from page" under it exactly as on the list's row; "Can edit their own responsibilities", a checkbox where the viewer holds Full
control and plain text otherwise; their Responsibilities (4.28) as written, or "Nothing written
yet.", with "Edit" where the viewer may write them, which opens one text box ("# heading",
"- bullet", two spaces per level, "**bold**", "_italic_", "[text](link)") with Cancel and
Save, the draft kept when someone saved first; and History, behind "Show", one list newest first of
their Access transitions and Responsibility revisions, each a sentence built from who acted,
on whom and what changed (AC-1240). The member's count of open tasks is not shown. Every
control is drawn from the details' `viewer_may_*` answers; the routes decide again.

The folder's icon and colour are the shared identity set from the mark; a member's personal
shelf placement is made by dragging the row onto a collection; 4.4's per-member colour column is written by
no v2 surface.

The cover picker offers three tiers on a shelf page (9.10) and one of 4.20's built-in
places, and on a folder — where "Add cover" appears above the title on hover and puts a flat
colour on the page in one press, and the band's own Change and Remove edit it afterwards:
**Colours** (a few flat colours that follow the light and dark theme; no gradients), **Upload** (stored, so it survives a reload) and
**Photos** (an openly licensed photograph the server fetches and stores, so the cover is a
file this origin serves). **No Link tier is drawn**: 10.8's `img-src` admits no external
host, so a pasted URL could not render, and a tier a surface cannot honestly draw is not
drawn rather than disabled. Where the picture is kept differs by kind and stays each
section's own — 7.4 for a folder (one look for every member), 7.16 for a shelf (private),
7.19 for a place (per viewer).
