### 9.6 Folder page

The folder page (`app/src/client/pages/FolderPage.tsx`) is a page in the full sense: a
cover, then a header — its mark (pressing it opens the icon-and-colour picker, writing 4.3's
shared identity) and its name (click to rename), with "Share" and one "…" at the right. There is no progress count ("N of M done"): nobody
measures a page by it, and a task's own subtask count stays on its row. There is no description line: the
Purpose tab holds a page's why, and a description already stored stays in the data, undrawn.
Then a row of plain text tabs (its facets; hidden ones are not drawn) and the tab's body.

Every page, at any depth, wears this same head; the page draws no breadcrumb of its own. A
page's path is the window bar's breadcrumb (9.1), which names only ancestors the viewer is a
member of (the server cuts the walk at the first one they are not). A page moves by dragging
its sidebar row, into another page or back to the top level; the "…" has no move item.

The tabs read intro, work, conclusion by default: **Purpose · Work · Results · Discussion**,
then custom tabs. `main` is shown as "Work" and `consultation` as "Discussion" (display names
from `DEFAULT_FACET_NAMES`; the stored kinds are unchanged). A page opens on Work. The default
order is only a default: tabs are reordered by dragging them on the row — a mouse press that
moves, or a long press on touch, lifts the tab, which follows the pointer at reduced opacity
while a thin accent line marks where it will land; Esc cancels; Alt+←/→ on a focused tab moves
it one place — written through `PUT /facets/order` (or `my-order` for a private tab), and once
a person has dragged, the stored order is the order. A quiet "+" after the last tab turns
into a name field: Enter makes a blank shared tab with that name and opens it; Esc cancels.
Right-clicking a tab offers Rename (in place) and, on a custom tab, Delete. A finished task
stays where it was placed, drawn as its own row, checked and muted; there is no separate
Done section and no "Completed Task" line. Ordering within sections and the block arrangement are the folder's, shared by every
member; the filter of 9.1 narrows the page without touching its arrangement. Desk, the
default folder, is drawn by this page like any other folder. A folder page is where
finished and abandoned tasks are seen, through "Show completed" in its "…"; no page
gathers them across folders.

While Work is the open tab, the right end of the tab row holds its view as three small icon
toggles, **Freeform** (the canvas, the default), **Linear** and **Board**, each named by its tooltip,
the current one on a muted tile; they are one Tab stop and the arrow keys move between them. The
view is remembered on the device apart from the place pages' layout, so choosing a page's view never
changes Assigned or Upcoming. "Show completed" sits in the page's "…", on Work and a
section's tab (on by default, remembered on the device).
Sections carry no "done/total" count; a task row's right side is one meta line (date in the
viewer's locale and clock, overdue in the danger ink, then subtask count, repeat glyph and a
light star), and clicking a row anywhere but its checkbox or star opens the task.
Show completed off hides only completed task placements in Freeform (their section and its
open tasks stay) and the Board's Done cards (the column stays, and still
takes a drop). **Linear** lists the page's tasks as GitHub Issues, in one bordered table. Its header row holds a select-all box; “Open”, “Closed” and “All”, each with its count under the header's filters (All shows done work in its place, never hidden); then Milestone (any, none, or one) and Assignee (anyone, unassigned, or one person) filters, a sort (“Page order”, the default, “Newest”, “Oldest”, “Recently updated”, “Due date”, “Most commented”), a Compact or Comfortable switch (Compact by default), and “New issue”. State, sort and density are remembered on the device. In page order a milestone (a section) is a row of the table with a diamond, its name and “3 of 7” closed with a thin bar; every milestone stays, empty or finished; tasks outside every section come first. Other sorts are one flat list. A row is one line: a select box, a drag handle (page order only), a state mark (open, in progress for a started task, closed, or struck for not planned), the title, the task's labels, and on the right the meta line, the comment count (none at zero) and the assignee's face; Comfortable adds “Opened 3 days ago by Maya”, “Closed yesterday” or “Closed as not planned 2 days ago” under the title. Dragging a row moves it within or across milestones in the page's Work document. With rows selected the header becomes “2 selected” with “Mark as” (Open, In progress, Done, Not planned), “Label”, “Assignee”, “Milestone” (to its end), “Delete” and a clear button. The table ends in the one “New issue, or type / for a milestone” line, which adds a task outside every section (before the first), into the chosen milestone under a Milestone filter, or with “/” a milestone; “New issue” in the header focuses it. Only the title opens the task as an issue in place (Back returns to the list); Enter or O opens the focused row, Space closes or reopens it, X selects it, Alt+arrows move it, C focuses New issue, and M and A open the Milestone and Assignee menus. The issue shows “Issues” (back), the title, a state pill (Open, Closed, Not planned) and “Maya opened this 3 days ago · 4 comments”; then the task's own document as the description (edited in place) with its subtasks, then its comments and its history merged oldest first, a run of the same history line shown once; then a comment box and “Close as not planned” and “Close as completed”, or “Reopen”. Not planned is abandoned. A side column (under the title on a phone) shows Assignee, Labels, Milestone and Dates, and “Edit task” opens the task where they are changed. **Board** has three columns, Not
started · In progress · Done, named in grey, blue and green pills with the count at the right,
and dropping a card in a column changes its status. Each column lists its cards in one fixed
order per stage, and a card carries the same meta line as a row. Not started and
In progress end with "New task", which adds a task in that status (In progress starts it
today); Done has none. Until the
server carries an In-progress state, In progress is derived: an open task whose start date is
today or earlier; a drop into In progress sets the start date to today (or the due date, if
that is earlier), a drop into Not started clears a past start date, a drop into Done
completes.

The folder menu is one list, `app/src/client/lib/object-acts.ts`, drawn identically by the
header's "…", the Library row's "…" and a right-click on that row: History, Rename, Copy
link, Duplicate, Import as sub-page…, Export as template, then Transfer ownership…, Leave page… or Delete page as the
viewer's role permits, with this page's "Show completed" beside History in its first group. **Export as template**
downloads this page as a Dana file (`<name>.dana.json`, 7.16's format) and says in one line what a template
leaves out (dates, done tasks, sub-pages, the discussion). **Import as sub-page…** chooses such a file and creates its
pages inside this one, named as the file names them ("(imported)" is added only when a sibling already has the name), as sub-pages that copy this page's members, in one transaction
(`POST /folders/:id/import`); it is offered at Can edit and above and never on Desk, which takes
no sub-pages. Import is the header's only. The header's "…" leaves Rename out: on the page the title is rename's one home,
renamed in place. Copy link copies silently, and Duplicate says so by landing on the copy.
Tabs are managed on the tab row alone: its "+" lists hidden tabs to show again above "New tab",
and "New tab" is offered nowhere else. A right-click on a tab (or Shift+F10) offers Rename and
Hide on Purpose and Results, Hide on Discussion, and Rename and Delete on custom tabs; Work,
whose name is fixed and which cannot be hidden, has no menu. Deleting a tab or a page is never confirmed: it
goes to Trash with Undo, and a deleted page lands on the page it sat in.

Clicking a task placement on the canvas (or Enter on it) opens the task as every list row
does. A placement has one menu, the gutter's ⋮⋮, whose Delete deletes the task (with the
row's confirmation and undo); the row's own "More actions" is not drawn on the canvas. A
failed save shows one quiet line, "Not saved — Retry", the line the Task and Shelf writers
show; a failure Retry cannot re-send (a conflict, or the page changed elsewhere) offers
"Load latest", which keeps the draft in the tab and offers it back, and no other error does.
A gesture the canvas refuses says why in that same line, never a second one, and the line
takes no height when it has nothing to say. Unsaved writing left by an earlier session is
offered back above the writing in the same words on every writer: "Unsaved writing from
before is kept." with Download writing, Restore (where it can be put back) and Discard.

The folder's conversation has one home: the Discussion tab (`consultation`), a list of threads
whose General discussion (which cannot be moved to Trash) is the folder's own. A link to the
conversation (`?conversation=open`) lands there and becomes the tab's own address (`?facet=<id>`), so the tab has one. The tab's name is heavier while any open thread has a post the reader has not read; nothing else marks it. The tab is two layers. The list is laid out as Today is: an "Open" group ending in "New thread", then a "Resolved" group (no tabs, no counts, no column headers; an old `?threads=` address is ignored). Each thread is one row at a task row's height, its status mark where a task's checkbox sits but never square: an outline speech bubble while open, a filled check circle once resolved; pressing it (or Space on the row) resolves the thread without a decision or reopens it, with Undo. The title carries the row, heavier when someone else has posted since the reader last read it (kept by the server per person, 4.34, so every device agrees); on a wide screen an open thread's last post follows its title, muted ("Bilal: How about Tuesday?", "You:" for the reader's own); on the right an open thread shows the faces of who else is posting (never the reader) and "4 posts · 12 min", a resolved one "Resolved 15 Sep". The list refreshes itself about every twenty seconds. An open thread is its own page: the page's head and tabs step aside, the top bar's breadcrumb names the thread and its page crumb leads back to the list, and the thread opens at the first post from someone else since the last read, with one "New" hairline above it for that visit, or else at its end, where on a desktop the cursor waits in the composer. Escape (from anywhere but a field holding writing or an open menu) goes back to the list, with focus on that thread's row. Its head is the page head's: the status (an outline speech bubble while open, a filled accent check once resolved) and the 28px title on the left, and "Resolve" or "Reopen" with a "…" menu (Rename, Move to Trash) on the right. The thread fills the window: its posts sit at the bottom just above the composer, the history scrolls in its own region, and the composer stays at the foot of the window; a grouped post shows its time in the gutter on hover. Move to Trash asks nothing: the list returns at once with an Undo. Then its posts oldest first, re-read every few seconds while the thread is open (a reader at the end stays at the end as posts arrive; one scrolled up gets a quiet "New posts" line above the composer instead), grouped as a chat groups them: a post by the same person within five minutes of their last, on the same day and not a reply to a post further up, draws only its text under the first one's face, name and time; a reply to a post further up carries one small line naming the post it answers (replying to a reply quotes the post clicked, and joins its thread's one level); a day divider appears only when the thread spans more than one day. Deleting a post keeps its replies (they become plain posts). A resolved thread ends in one quiet line instead of the composer, "Resolved <date> by <name>" and its decision if one was given; Reopen is in the head. A post is written in the canvas's editor (headings, lists, checklists, quotes, code, images), less the pages-only blocks; its empty box says "Write a reply…" ("Write the first post…" in an empty thread); Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line (in a list, a code block, an open menu or on a touch screen Enter stays the editor's, and Ctrl/Cmd+Enter or the send button posts). A sent post leaves the box at once and shows in the thread at once, muted until the server has it, so whatever is typed meanwhile stays and a second Enter sends a second post after the first. The members of a page never share a face's colour there, and a mention reads as plain "@Name" on the line, tinted, with no face. "Resolve" puts an optional "What was decided?" line where the composer was, in the composer's own box with "Resolve" where Send was; likes are the whole reaction set.

The body is a BlockNote editor; `app/src/client/components/page/canvas/` is the truth for
the surface, and `app/src/client/components/page/canvas/schema.ts` fixes the vocabulary (prose, Section headings, images,
real task rows of 4.17; bold and italic). Two things are contract rather than surface:

- **A Section is a semantic boundary**: it owns the items nested under it, and moving a task
  across one changes the task's Section. It is drawn as a plain heading, as in Notion: no
  arrow, no fold, and what it holds sits at its own indent. A heading that folds is a toggle
  heading, which is writing and owns no Task. "Heading n" and `#` make the same Section where it
  is typed: inside a Section of that level or deeper it steps out to its level and takes what
  followed it along, so nothing moves on screen; an empty line becomes the Section itself.
- **A Task is made in place from a block**: "Task" in the `/` menu or "Turn into task" on
  the block turns a line with prose straight into a Task titled by that prose, keeping the
  block's position; on an empty line the line itself becomes the task being titled (a
checkbox and "Task title"), created on Enter or on leaving it, and removed if left empty.
  Enter or Backspace on it while it is still empty ends it as on an empty to-do in Notion: the
  line stays, plain text, with the caret on it.
  `app/src/client/components/page/canvas/CanvasSurface.tsx` carries the routes. The `/`
  menu (`slash-items.tsx`) lists Text, Heading 1–3, Bulleted list, Numbered list, Toggle list,
  Quote, Callout, Divider, Code, Image, File, Task, Page, Diagram and Link to…, each row an
  icon, its name and the markdown shortcut for it; Toggle heading 1–3 are found by typing.
  There is no To-do list: Task is the checkbox. An empty page says "Write, or type / for a task
  or heading" until it is clicked (an empty Purpose says "Write why this page exists", an empty Results
"Write what came of this, or type / for a Result", and a reader who cannot write sees neither); while writing, only the focused empty block shows a muted
  hint ("Press '/' for commands", "Heading", "List", or "What came of it" in a Result), gone at the first character, and saving says nothing — no "Saving…", no "Saved" — until a save fails.

Blocks are selected as in Notion (`app/src/client/components/page/canvas/CanvasMarquee.tsx`,
every canvas and the Task composer): dragging from empty space or the margins draws a faint
rectangle and the blocks it touches become selected, tinted; Shift-click extends, Esc in text
selects the block and Esc again clears, Cmd/Ctrl+A selects the block's text and then every
block. Selected blocks are deleted together, duplicated (Cmd/Ctrl+D), turned into another kind
(Cmd/Ctrl+/), nested (Tab/Shift-Tab) and dragged together by any one's handle. Arrow keys
move the selection a block, Shift+arrows extend it, and Enter goes back to writing at the
end of the block. The tint is one soft rounded shape a little larger than the run of
blocks. Tasks and Folder rows are never duplicated, and the guard still refuses a deletion
that holds a Task. A click in the space below the last block (the lower part of the screen,
in every editor) writes on: into the last block if it is an empty line, otherwise into a
new line after it.

Video links embed only through YouTube's privacy-enhanced player and Vimeo's player (10.8's
`frame-src` admits nothing else); direct media URLs stay ordinary external links, and every
recognised URL is a semantic external link.

Who may do what is the Access ladder of 5.4, resolved by `app/src/shared/folder-actions.ts`,
which the routes themselves consult; the client reads it through
`app/src/client/lib/folder-authority.ts` and states no bar of its own. A surface offers
exactly the acts the viewer's Access permits — Leave where Delete would be, never both —
and the server refuses the rest regardless (10.2). Shelf placement, sidebar arrangement
and label colour are always personal.

- **Delete** moves the folder to Trash for every member at once, with the undo bar and no
  confirmation; members are notified (4.14). An owner with other members is asked nothing
  either (AC-283): handing the page on first is Transfer ownership in the "…", and the undo
  bar is the way back.
- **Leave** confirms, because the way back is an invitation rather than a Trash row; it
  writes no Trash entry, offers no undo, and records "Left". The confirmation says access is
  lost and anything assigned to the leaver becomes unassigned.
- **An owner with other members leaves only by transferring ownership first** (7.16),
  choosing a successor from the members who currently hold Full control, with no default
  among several and no order that means anything: joining first is no evidence anybody
  intended you to hold custody. A single candidate is already picked. Where no member holds
  Full control the picker says so and points at Share rather than presenting an empty list.
  The dialog says only the new owner can hand it back, and a toast names the new owner once
  it is done. A sole owner is offered Delete instead (5.3).
- Removing a member confirms (`app/src/client/pages/FolderSharingPanel.tsx`). Each confirmation is one
  sentence.

Duplicate takes the structure and never the grants or the progress: tasks, sections,
paragraphs and section assignments copy; members, sharing, the conversation, assignees and
completion do not; spent due dates re-anchor on today and future ones keep, and the bar
says which happened. The copy is the duplicator's own folder, and its tasks are
assigned to whoever made it, so the copy appears in their Assignments. It lands right
after the original: a sub-page's copy under the same parent (a sub-page like any other,
with that parent's members) when the duplicator may add pages there, otherwise a
top-level page placed after the original in their Library and its collection. The History sheet
groups by day and names the actor on every line, through the same formatter as the row's
popover (`app/src/client/lib/history.ts`).

The sharing panel (`app/src/client/pages/FolderSharingPanel.tsx`) has one "Name or email"
field, the Access the invite grants (the six presets, back to Can edit after each invite), and one
"Invite" button: the field suggests connections who are not yet members, and
Invite sends a page invite to the picked connection's address or the typed one, which the
server turns into an instant share when the address is already a connection (7.5). Below it
are the members, each with their Access ("Owner" for the owner): where the viewer may change
it, a menu of the six presets on the row, with "Remove from page" under them where 5.4 allows
removal (confirmed, never on the owner's row or the viewer's own, since leaving is Leave
page…). On the viewer's own row, marked "(you)", a change of Access confirms first, because
it drops them below Full control and they cannot raise it back. A viewer who may not share
sees the panel titled "Members of" the page rather than "Share", and the header button that opens it reads "Members". Then the pending invites, each with the Access it
grants and when it was sent, "Cancel", and a Resend shown only once 24 hours have passed since the last send. It opens from the "Share" button in the
folder's header; Share is withheld on the default folder, which INV-2 makes single-member.

A member's name in that list opens their details in the same panel
(`app/src/client/pages/MemberDetails.tsx`, #209), with "All members" to go back: their Access,
a menu of the six presets where the viewer may change it (never on the owner's row, and sent
with the Access the panel showed, so a change made meanwhile is refused rather than
replaced), with "Remove from page" under it exactly as on the list's row; "Can edit their own responsibilities", a checkbox where the viewer holds Full
control and plain text otherwise; their Responsibilities (4.28) as written, or "Nothing written
yet.", with "Edit" where the viewer may write them, which opens one text box ("# heading",
"- bullet", two spaces per level, "**bold**", "_italic_", "[text](link)") with Cancel and
Save, the draft kept when someone saved first; and History, behind "Show", one list newest first of
their Access transitions and Responsibility revisions, each a sentence built from who acted,
on whom and what changed (AC-1240). The member's count of open tasks is not shown. Every
control is drawn from the details' `viewer_may_*` answers; the routes decide again.

The folder's icon and colour are the shared identity set from the mark; a member's personal
shelf placement is made by dragging the row onto a collection; 4.4's per-member colour column is written by
no v2 surface.

The cover picker offers three tiers on a shelf page (9.10) and one of 4.20's built-in
places, and on a folder — where "Add cover" appears above the title on hover and puts a flat
colour on the page in one press, and the band's own Change and Remove edit it afterwards:
**Colours** (a few flat colours that follow the light and dark theme; no gradients), **Upload** (stored, so it survives a reload) and
**Photos** (an openly licensed photograph the server fetches and stores, so the cover is a
file this origin serves). **No Link tier is drawn**: 10.8's `img-src` admits no external
host, so a pasted URL could not render, and a tier a surface cannot honestly draw is not
drawn rather than disabled. Where the picture is kept differs by kind and stays each
section's own — 7.4 for a folder (one look for every member), 7.16 for a shelf (private),
7.19 for a place (per viewer).
