### 9.10 Shelves

A shelf is the folder page one level up (`app/src/client/screens/shelf-page.tsx`): a
per-user arrangement of folders with a page of its own — cover, mark, name, description,
and blocks. Renaming rewrites the member labels and re-addresses the page in one act.
Folder rows appear on the page as blocks among the writing: their presence is the
membership's fact, their position the page's arrangement; a folder row under a heading
folds with it; pressing one opens that folder. A row is the folder's mark and name and
nothing else. Folders join a shelf only from the Library — dragging a folder into the
shelf's group, or the folder's "Move to shelf" — and the page offers no picker of its own;
an arrival is placed after the page's writing. Rename lives in the page's title (a bare
label with no page renames from its Library heading). Save status shows only when
unsaved for more than two seconds or failed, never a lone "Saved". The shelf's acts are declared in
`app/src/client/lib/object-acts.ts`. Deleting a shelf asks first, in the sidebar's sentence, then unlabels its folders and
keeps them. The page's header is two rows: mark, name and one "…" menu, then the
description.

A shelf offers no views and no filter: a view redraws tasks and a shelf holds folders; its
list is its label, and narrowing it would be editing it. Shelves are private by
construction — no members, and no link grants anything — though the page's address is
copyable, an address not being an invitation.

A cover already chosen still draws at the top of the page; the page offers no control to
add one. A shelf's picture is kept in 4.18's
columns through 7.16's PATCH, private like the rest of the shelf.

Duplicate is one call, `POST /api/v1/shelves/:id/duplicate` (7.16). The copy separates the
shelf from its membership: no folders (a folder is on one shelf) and none of the shelf's
blocks. It is named "{name} (copy)", then "(copy 2)" upward where that name is taken, since
4.18 admits one shelf per name per user. It carries the cover as a picture of its own, not
a second reference to the same file, so changing or removing either shelf's cover leaves
the other alone.
