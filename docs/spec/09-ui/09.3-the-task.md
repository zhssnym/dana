### 9.3 The task (create and edit are one artifact, and the artifact is the task)

There is no task form. A task is its row, its card, and its page: the row expands in place
into the card; the card is the same surface a dialog, a side panel, or a tab presents; and
/app/tasks/:id is that surface as a page. Every edit commits as it is made — there is no
Save control and no draft state — and the undo bar of 9.1 covers the destructive ones.
Where a task opens (dialog, panel, or tab) is a client-side preference; a row offers the
choice and remembers it, and "tab" opens the task at /app/tasks/:id, which is the card as
a page (polish round, 2026-08-30: the setting accepted "tab" and opened the dialog
regardless, over a read-only page whose one button popped that same dialog; two of the
round's fixers reached this sentence, one proposing that the third option go and one
building what it promised, and the built one is what shipped, so 9.7's Appearance control
still names three). The side panel makes the page narrower rather than covering it. A task
opened as a tab carries its own window-bar controls, and its folder and section stand at
the bar's left.

The card holds, in order: the title (editable in place, required); a header strip of chips
— due date, folder, assignee, reminder, repeat — each opening its picker in place; then
Steps, Notes, and Attachments as blocks; then Comments (9.6's conversation rules apply);
then a footer toolbar carrying the completion control, the star, and "More actions"
(History, Copy link, Duplicate, Move to folder, Make this a folder, Abandon or Restore,
Delete). "Delete task…" confirms with "Move this task to Trash? It stays there for 30
days." (the selection bar counts its plural), and the footer renders the task's creation
and its completion; the rest of its history is the Activity control (polish round,
2026-08-30). The pickers open on the answer, not the instrument: resolved presets first, a
calendar or list behind them, one shape at one width.

Field rules:

| Field | Rule |
| - | - |
| title | 1 to 300; empty commits are refused with "Give it a title" |
| due date | a day; the due picker shows its grid plainly and carries a Time field under it that writes 4.8's reminder on that day rather than a due_time (polish round, 2026-08-30); a stored due_time from v1 is displayed and clearable, never settable |
| reminder | requires a due date; one picker, a time on that day, writing 4.8's time_of_day mode; disabled helper "Set a due date first" |
| repeat | requires a due date; preset-driven exactly as v1 (daily, weekdays, weekly on chosen days, every N days or weeks, monthly on day 1 to 28, yearly, optional "Ends on"); never a raw RRULE box; same helper |
| folder | the real folder library plus "New folder…" |
| assignee | the target folder's members plus "Invite by email", and — new in v2, closing section 13 item 28 — "Unassigned", offered last after a separator; hidden in single-member folders (INV-5) |
| steps | add, check, delete, reorder by drag or keyboard; cap notice "Limit 50 steps" |
| notes | up to 10000 |
| attachments | files and links per 4.16 and 10.3; a pasted URL becomes a link, never a fake file |

Row and card decisions carried from `prototype-v2` (RFC 0005 change control item 57, D). A
row carries its section after its folder chip, read as a path ("Travel › Venue &
vendors"), and omits it where the row already sits under that section's heading (L-153).
Two densities, one component: two lines is the default because a truncated title does not
announce itself where fewer rows do; the two shapes may differ in height and in where the
details sit and in nothing else; a bare task costs one line (L-076, L-077, L-148, L-150).
The details on a row are the controls that change them: the due and repeat chips open the
same pickers the card opens (L-151). One model turns a task's fields into words for the
row and the card alike, and a person's tint is deterministic from the model so one person
is one colour on every surface (L-070, L-071). Wherever a person is drawn — a row's
assignee chip, a card's, a board's, a group heading, a roster row, a member stack, a
sharing panel, a comment, an invite, a notification, the account button — their own
picture is what is drawn, and the tinted initials are the fallback for a person who has
none. (Amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30, avatars F2. Every one of those reads has
carried `avatar_url` since 4.1 and the face component has taken an image since it was
shared; what was missing was that most call sites never passed one, so the same person
wore a photograph on a card and a coloured disc on the row beneath it.) A row's own rename
handler, where a surface supplies one, wins over the card's (L-074); the row's title is
that handler's control, so pressing it renames in place, committing on Enter or on blur
and abandoning on Escape (DES-095, L-152). That last clause reverses item 47(e)'s
exemption, which read "on the shipped row the title is text and renaming is the card's":
the owner asked for the prototype's behaviour at the polish round of 2026-08-30, and the
exemption is spent rather than merely unused. Two consequences follow and neither is
optional. 9.4's "a click on any row opens that task" now means the row and not its title,
which is the reading the due and folder chips have always had, a chip opening its own
picker rather than the task; the gestures that open a task from a row it renames in are a
press anywhere else on it and the chevron. And a browser criterion that opens a task by
pressing its title text, or that addresses a control inside a row by an accessible name a
task title can contain, no longer discriminates what it was written to discriminate; those
are persona 11's under protocol section 8 and the round raised them rather than editing
them. An empty block draws the control that fills it rather than nothing (L-157). The
surface's own strip offers the tab only where the surface can hand the task to the page
bar, a tab being an action rather than a state (L-037); Escape closes the surface and its
chrome, and the card's footer offers no second close (L-163).

Clearing the date keeps 8.4's warning verbatim: "Clearing the date also ends the repeat
and removes the reminder." Moving keeps the unassign warning: "Moving will unassign Sara;
she isn't a member of Home." Deleting moves the task to Trash with the undo bar, and no
confirmation: two ways back is why the question left. The completion control and the
history read stay: the footer renders the task's own events from 4.13 — created,
completed, and the rest — actor and viewer-local time, "Deleted user" fallback unchanged.

Entry defaults, derived from the page's own spine so a made task appears where it was
made:

| Opened from | defaults |
| - | - |
| Desk, All, or the bare strip control | folder Desk, undated |
| Today | folder Desk, a Today entry (membership), dated today |
| Scheduled list | folder Desk, dated today |
| A calendar cell | folder Desk, dated that cell's day |
| Important | folder Desk, important on |
| Assignments | folder Desk, assigned to the viewer |
| A folder page, its loose block | that folder |
| A section's capture | that folder, that section |
| A board column | that column's value, whatever its axis — folder, person, day, or section |

Board and section decisions carried from `prototype-v2` (item 57, D): a board lays two
columns per row; a section may carry a mark, a folder's glyph or a person's face, inline
in its heading so a section without one simply starts its name earlier; the count sits
quietly beside the name; Overdue refuses a drop, being a relation to today rather than a
value (L-170, L-172, L-173, L-219).

Validation copy is v1's: "Give it a title", "Too long: limit N characters", 422 with the
field named.
