### 8.10 Account deletion

One transaction, ordered so the RESTRICT tripwire on folders.owner_id can never fire:

```
1  set status='invalidated' on the user's pending sent invites
2  for each owned non-default folder with other members: transfer ownership (8.9)
3  for each owned folder now without other members, plus the default folder:
       delete the folder row outright, taking 8.8's purge cascades here and
       not 8.8's soft delete: the account is going, so there is no Trash
       left for it to wait in
4  for each remaining membership in others' folders: run R-CLEAN, delete membership
5  delete all connection rows containing the user
6  delete remaining my_day_entries of the user
6a collect the files rows step 3's cascades dropped and every files row still
       owned by the user, the avatar's folderless row included; delete the
       remaining rows here, and remove every collected object from the store
       only after the transaction commits, the same order 7.15's delete uses.
       A folder transferred away at step 2 keeps its files.
6c delete the user's rows in the nine personal tables no cascade can reach:
       comment_likes, labels (scope 'personal', by owner_user_id),
       notifications, sample_objects, shelves, user_page_covers,
       user_page_descriptions, user_settings, user_views
7  scrub the user row: google_sub, email, display_name, avatar_url set null; deleted_at = now()
8  destroy all sessions of the user
```

Step 6a was added 2026-08-25 by RFC 0005 change control item 18: files (4.16) arrived
after this section was written, and without the step the deleted account's avatar stayed
readable at its published id and every upload stayed in the store indefinitely, which
broke this section's promise.

**Step 6c was added 2026-09-20 by issue #253, and the general fact underneath it is the
part worth carrying away.** Step 7 scrubs the `users` row rather than deleting it, and
**a row that is never deleted fires no `ON DELETE` clause — not `CASCADE`, not `SET NULL`,
not `RESTRICT` — so a table that keyed its cleanup to `users` is not cleaned up at all,
and nothing anywhere reports it.** That wording is issue #230's, which met the same class
on `folder_responsibilities` on the same day and put it better than #253 did.

Read the three separately, because only two of them cost anything. A `CASCADE` on
`users(id)` promises to take the departing person's rows and takes none: that is this
section's defect and 6c is the answer to it. A `SET NULL` would promise to release a
column and would leave it pointing at the scrubbed account: the same silence, and there is
no such key in this schema today, which is precisely why the next one will be written by
somebody who assumes it works. A `RESTRICT` or `NO ACTION` is the harmless one and is the
schema doing what this section wants — `tasks.created_by` keeps naming the scrubbed row on
purpose. **So the direction that fails silently is the one that has to be argued for**, and
the guard below enumerates both of the first two.

This section performs no `DELETE` on that row — deliberately, so that the surviving
references in the paragraph below keep resolving — and therefore any table that expects
one to clear a departing person's rows keeps them indefinitely, with no error and nothing
to notice. **The next table added with `REFERENCES users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE` will not
work either**, and the cost of assuming otherwise is measured three times over in one day:
#227's own issue text said Personal Labels leave "through ownership cascade" and they would
have outlived every account that made them — **which stopped being a hypothetical while
this branch waited**: issue #226 landed 4.24's `labels` with exactly that key, the guard
below went red on it at the rebase, and `db/migrations/0022_labels.sql` carried a comment
beside the constraint saying those rows "go with their account (8.10)" when nothing took
them; #230 found that a deleted account would have
kept an **open** `folder_responsibilities` row, `ended_at IS NULL`, in every folder it was
ever in, still answered by `activeStandingOf` as a live member Responsibility and still
holding a partial unique index against whoever came next; and this issue is the other
nine.

Fourteen such keys exist in section 4's schema and 10.1's `sessions` is a fifteenth
outside it. Five were already handled — `connections` twice at step 5, `my_day_entries` at
step 6, `folder_members` by step 3's folder deletes and step 4's R-CLEAN, `sessions` by
step 8 — and the other nine are 6c, which is why 6c is a list of tables rather than a
rule: there is no rule, only the enumeration and an argument per table.
`USER_CASCADE_DISPOSALS` in `app/src/server/domain/account-deletion.ts` carries that
argument for all fifteen, including the one deliberate retention, and
`app/tests/guards/guard-account-deletion-sweep.test.ts` fails a sixteenth key that nothing
names. **The honest reading of a `CASCADE` on `users(id)` in this schema is "this key
documents intent and enforces nothing for account deletion"**; whether `RESTRICT`, which
would at least fail loudly rather than silently, is the better default for a new
user-referencing key is issue #253's fourth question and is not settled here.

`page_document_receipts` is deliberately kept, alone among the fifteen. Its rows carry no
text — an idempotency key, a payload hash, a revision and task ids — and a folder's
receipts are read a second way, as the mark that its document is on the transaction path
of #184, so removing a departed member's could change how the folder behaves for the
people still in it. Its `folder_id` key is a cascade off a real `DELETE`, so step 3
already takes the receipts of every folder that dies with the account.

**The fact was already written down twice, for one table each, and never generalised** —
4.2 says of `connections` that "users are soft-deleted, so the CASCADE never fires in
practice", and 10.1 says of `sessions` that its key is "a cascade that never fires in
production". Both are correct and both read as a remark about their own table. Nothing
said it was true of *every* such key, which is why the nine that nobody had written a
remark about were the nine that leaked — the ninth being `labels`, whose migration did
write a remark and wrote the opposite one.

**What this makes true again is a sentence a person reads.** The Privacy page
(`app/src/client/pages/LegalPage.tsx`) says "Deleting your account permanently removes
your tasks and your personal data from the running product". Until 6c that sentence was
false — shelves by name, saved Views with their criteria, preferences, notification
history, page dressing, likes and Personal Labels all stayed — and no criterion could have caught it,
each of AC-133 to AC-138 being scoped to its own subject and correct within it. Whoever
weakens 6c is weakening that sentence, and should move the copy in the same change.

**The lettering is 6a and 6c, and 6b is a deliberate gap.** It was reserved for issue
#227, whose branch was open while this one was and was to add the Personal Label delete at
that number. **That reservation is spent rather than waiting**: #226 landed `labels` on
`main` first, this branch rebased onto it, and the Personal Label delete is one of 6c's
nine statements, scoped by `owner_user_id`. A rebasing #227 therefore adds no 6b for
`labels`; what it owes instead is a disposal entry for any *further* user-referencing
table it introduces, which the guard will demand of it by name. The gap in the lettering
is left as it is, because renumbering a step this section is cited by costs more than a
missing letter does.

**A departed member's Responsibility record is retained, deliberately, and this is the
decision rather than an omission** (issue #230, on the accepted technical ruling
https://github.com/zhssnym/dana/issues/230#issuecomment-5751322947). Step 4's R-CLEAN
**closes** the person's standing in each folder they were in — `folder_responsibilities`
gets its `ended_at`, and a `responsibility_ended` entry records it — and the row, its
content and every revision of it in `folder_responsibility_history` **survive and stay
readable to that folder's current members**. The authority is #209 R10: history survives
the membership it describes. The two `users` keys on each table are `RESTRICT` rather than
`CASCADE` for that reason and are outside 6c's sweep by design, which is the one place in
this section where a `RESTRICT` is load-bearing rather than merely harmless.

**It does not contradict the Privacy page sentence above.** That sentence is about the
person's own tasks and personal data; a Responsibility is the *folder's* record of one
person's part in its shared work, written and read by the folder, and taking it away would
edit other people's history of what was done rather than delete the departing person's
data. The text of it carries no contact details, and the departed account renders as
"Deleted user" beside it like every other surviving reference. What does go is the live
standing: the person is no longer a member, the document is closed, and nothing about them
is answered as current anywhere.

Surviving references (tasks.created_by, tasks.completed_by, invites.accepted_by) keep
pointing at the scrubbed row and render as "Deleted user".

The sentence that used to close this section, "Google requires apps offering account
creation to offer deletion; verify the current policy text before launch", named the wrong
rulebook and its check was never done at launch. It was done on 2026-09-03 and the answer
is 10.9: no Google policy binding Dana requires account deletion at all, the rule it
half-remembered being Google Play's and Dana being on no store. Dana offers deletion
because the product should, which is a stronger reason than a policy and does not move
when a policy does.
