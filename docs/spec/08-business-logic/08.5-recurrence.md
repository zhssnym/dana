### 8.5 Recurrence

**Evaluation.** A rule string is always evaluated with DTSTART = the current occurrence's
due_date (at due_time or 00:00). nextInstance(rule, anchor, after) returns the earliest
rule instance whose date is strictly after the date "after", or null when UNTIL bounds it
out. UNTIL is inclusive per RFC 5545: an instance equal to UNTIL is valid; the next one is
not. Implementation: `app/src/server/time/recurrence.ts`.

**Generation** runs inside the same transaction as a pending-to-completed or
pending-to-abandoned transition when recurrence_rule is set. Implementation:
`generateSuccessor` in `app/src/server/time/generation.ts`. The rules:

- cutoff = the later of t.due_date and today in the tz of the request that made the
  transition (8.1), never the actor's stored users.timezone. The next due date is
  nextInstance(rule, anchor = t.due_date, after = cutoff).
- When UNTIL is exhausted nothing is generated and the rule stays on t, so the series may
  resume via restore.
- Otherwise a pending successor is inserted in the same folder and series with the same
  title, notes, assignee, importance, due_time, rule and created_by; t's steps are copied
  in order, not done; a reminder on t is copied with the same mode and value, its fire_at
  and state per 8.6. Today entries are never copied.
- The rule then moves: t.recurrence_rule becomes null, because the rule lives only on the
  current occurrence.

Worked consequences, with a weekly Monday rule:

- On time: due 2026-07-20, completed 2026-07-20. cutoff 07-20, next 2026-07-27.
- Late, slots skipped: due 2026-07-20, completed 2026-07-28. cutoff 07-28, next
  2026-08-03; the 07-27 slot is skipped and the calendar shows an honest gap.
- Early, alignment kept: due 2026-07-27, completed 2026-07-25. cutoff 07-27, next
  2026-08-03.
- Daily late, no pile-up: daily due 2026-07-20, completed 2026-07-24. cutoff 07-24, next
  2026-07-25.

Nothing is ever generated by the passage of time; a missed occurrence just snowballs in
Today until acted on. There is no recurrence background job.

**Un-complete and restore** apply the G-SERIES guard of 5.1: delete the pending successor
and reclaim the rule, or, when this row is the newest of its series, reopen it as the
current occurrence; otherwise 409.

**Editing mid-series.** The rule exists only on the current occurrence, so editing
recurrence_rule there changes the series from now on; completed and abandoned rows are
historical and untouched. Changing the current occurrence's due_date re-anchors the series
(the next generation evaluates from the new date). Setting the rule to null ends the
series; the row remains a normal dated task. Setting a rule on a task that never had one
requires a due_date and mints a fresh series_id.

**Calendar projection** (`virtualInstances` in `app/src/server/time/projection.ts`): only
a pending task with a rule projects, and its virtual instances are the rule's dates
(anchored on its due_date) that fall inside the requested range and strictly after its
due_date. Real rows draw on their own dates in any status; ghosts appear only after the
current occurrence's date; therefore no date can ever show the same series twice, and no
past date shows an occurrence that never existed. Clicking a ghost opens the current
occurrence.
