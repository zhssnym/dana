### 10.1 Authentication and sessions

Google OAuth 2.0 with OpenID Connect, scopes openid email profile only; no Gmail or
Calendar scopes exist anywhere in v1. All three are non-sensitive, which is what keeps
Dana out of Google's Limited Use restrictions and its annual security assessment; 10.9
records what the platform does require and what asking for a fourth scope would cost.
Sessions are server-side, referenced by an httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie, 30-day
rolling expiry; mutations additionally require a CSRF token when cookies are the carrier.
Sign-out destroys the session; account deletion destroys all sessions (8.10). Sign-ins
with an unverified Google email are rejected with an explanatory page (copy in 9.2).

Session storage is a dedicated sessions table, hand-rolled with the rest of the auth
module (11.6), outside the frozen section 4 model. Its shape is the Security engineer's,
except four facts fixed here as contract, the first three so the acceptance suite can
observe expiry (AC-113) and shared-database test runs can clean up, the fourth so the
migration and the module could be authored by their separate owners without a guess: the
table is named sessions; it references users(id) through a user_id column declared ON
DELETE CASCADE (a cascade that never fires in production, where account deletion scrubs
the user row and destroys sessions explicitly per 8.10; it exists so test teardown that
hard-deletes users cannot strand rows); expiry is a timestamptz column named expires_at,
which every authenticated request resets to 30 days from that request's time; and the
lookup key is a token_hash text column, the table's primary key, holding the lowercase hex
sha256 digest of the cookie's CSPRNG token, so the raw identifier exists nowhere at rest.
user_id and expires_at are NOT NULL, transcription of this section's own sentences: a
session always belongs to a user and always expires. The per-request write is deliberate:
it is what makes AC-113's rolling half observable, and its cost is noise at v1 scale on
one instance; throttling the extension is an optimization that must amend this sentence
and AC-113 together. The DDL is forward-only migrations in app/db/migrations like every
table, authored by the Data engineer to this contract; anything further the module needs
(a stored CSRF binding, timestamps) is requested precisely by the Security engineer and
transcribed the same way. The table stays out of the section 4 constraint catalog that
AC-001 asserts: the catalog test admits it as this section's one named exception.

**Dana never joins two accounts because their email addresses look alike.** Ruled by
Imran on 2026-09-03 against issue #17, which had been parked since August waiting for it
to happen to somebody, and which section 13 carried as items 1 and 19.

Account identity is the Google subject, `users.google_sub`, and nothing else. Provisioning
is idempotent under that column alone (7.1), and `users_email_active_idx` makes an address
unique across live accounts, so a Google subject Dana has never seen, carrying a verified
email a live account already holds, cannot be provisioned. **It is refused, and the
refusal renders an explanatory page (copy in 9.2) rather than returning to the landing
page with the bare failure marker.** No rows are created, nothing is linked, nothing is
overwritten, and no session is established. This is what already happened; what changed on
that date is that the person is told why.

Of the three available answers only refusal was acceptable. Joining the two accounts
automatically is the friendliest and is the only one that can hand somebody another
person's account, and it cannot be undone once it has. Asking is a screen nobody has
designed and would still have to refuse the person who answers wrong. Refusing costs a
real person a sign-in and costs nobody their data, which is the trade this section makes
everywhere else.

**The Gmail dotted-variant question is deferred by the same ruling, and the current
behaviour is specified rather than left accidental.** Dana performs no address
normalisation of any kind: matching is byte equality on the lowercased address, in 8.7 for
invites and in this section for accounts, so `imran.wafa@gmail.com` and
`imranwafa@gmail.com` are two addresses here and one mailbox at Google. Two consequences
follow and both are current behaviour. An invite sent to one spelling is not waiting when
the person signs in at the other, and 8.7's repair is the inviter's own cancel and resend
through the visible pending list. And a second Google account at the dotted spelling of an
address Dana already holds is a different address, so it provisions a second account
rather than colliding; it is only when the two spellings are identical that the refusal
above fires. Normalisation is not added until there is a secure technical answer, because
the naive one, stripping dots from every address, is wrong for every domain but Gmail and
would silently merge two people at a domain that treats dots as significant. AC-064
asserts the invite half and AC-346 the account half.
