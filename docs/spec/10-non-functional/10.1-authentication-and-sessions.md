### 10.1 Authentication and sessions

Google OAuth 2.0 with OpenID Connect, scopes openid email profile only; no Gmail or
Calendar scopes exist anywhere. All three are non-sensitive, which is what keeps Dana out
of Google's Limited Use restrictions and its annual security assessment; 10.9 records what
the platform does require and what asking for a fourth scope would cost.
Sessions are server-side, referenced by an httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookie, 30-day
rolling expiry; mutations additionally require a CSRF token when cookies are the carrier.
Sign-out destroys the session; account deletion destroys all sessions (8.10). Sign-ins
with an unverified Google email are rejected with an explanatory page (copy in 9.2).
The session cookie is host-only — no Domain attribute is set and none should be — so a
session exists on the one address it was established on and nowhere else, which is why 11.11
requires this deployment to answer on exactly one host and to redirect every other one to it.

Session storage is a dedicated `sessions` table, hand-rolled with the rest of the auth
module (11.6), outside the section 4 model and outside the section 4 constraint catalog.
Columns and constraints: `app/db/migrations/0001_sessions.sql` and
`app/db/migrations/0002_sessions_token_hash.sql`. What the table must mean:

- The lookup key is the lowercase hex sha256 digest of the cookie's CSPRNG token, so the raw
  identifier exists nowhere at rest.
- A session always belongs to a user and always expires. Every authenticated request resets
  its expiry to 30 days from that request's time; the per-request write is deliberate, being
  what makes the expiry rolling, and its cost is noise at Dana's scale on one instance.
- The foreign key to `users` cascades on delete. The cascade never fires in production, where
  account deletion scrubs the user row and destroys sessions explicitly (8.10); it exists so
  that test teardown hard-deleting users cannot strand rows.

**Dana never joins two accounts because their email addresses look alike.** Account
identity is the Google subject, `users.google_sub`, and nothing else. Provisioning is
idempotent under that column alone (7.1), and `users_email_active_idx` makes an address
unique across live accounts, so a Google subject Dana has never seen, carrying a verified
email a live account already holds, cannot be provisioned. **It is refused, and the refusal
renders an explanatory page (copy in 9.2).** No rows are created, nothing is linked,
nothing is overwritten, and no session is established.

Refusal is the only acceptable answer. Joining the accounts automatically can hand somebody
another person's account and cannot be undone once it has; asking would still have to refuse
the person who answers wrong. Refusing costs a real person a sign-in and costs nobody their
data.

**Dana performs no address normalisation of any kind.** Matching is byte equality on the
lowercased address, in 8.7 for invites and in this section for accounts, so
`imran.wafa@gmail.com` and `imranwafa@gmail.com` are two addresses here and one mailbox at
Google. An invite sent to one spelling is not waiting when the person signs in at the other,
and 8.7's repair is the inviter's own cancel and resend. A second Google account at the
dotted spelling of an address Dana already holds is a different address, so it provisions a
second account rather than colliding; the refusal above fires only when the two spellings
are identical. Normalisation waits for a secure technical answer, because the naive one,
stripping dots from every address, is wrong for every domain but Gmail and would silently
merge two people at a domain that treats dots as significant.
