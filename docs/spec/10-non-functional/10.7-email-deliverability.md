### 10.7 Email deliverability

One transactional provider serves invite, share-grant, and reminder templates. Launch
checklist: sending domain with SPF, DKIM, DMARC configured; from-address noreply@ the
product domain; short templates with one action link each. This is a named operational
setup cost, done once.

**Templates are HTML with a required text/plain alternative part**, amended 2026-08-25 on
Imran's ruling (RFC 0005 change control item 26; this citation read 25 until S19 corrected
it at item 34, item 25 being the 9.3 reunion's acceptance surface), where this sentence
previously read "plain, short templates". Both halves are load-bearing and neither is
optional: a send that carries HTML alone does not meet this section, and the text part
must say the same thing and carry the same one action link rather than being a stub.
"Short" and "one action link each" are unchanged and are the halves that do the
deliverability work. The reason the plain-text rule was dropped: what decides inbox
placement is the authentication this same sentence already requires, plus sender
reputation and the text-to-link ratio, and well-formed multipart with a text alternative
is what every transactional provider sends, so the rule was buying little and cost the
whole of the designed email surface.

**Rationale for where the orientation footer goes, 2026-09-21, folded from RFC 0005 under
the technical ruling of that date**
(https://github.com/zhssnym/dana/issues/280#issuecomment-5754491849, issue #280). It
changes no rule above; only the reasoning moves.

The footer goes on the two invitations and on neither of the other two, and that boundary
is a deliverability rule rather than a matter of taste. The transactional exemptions in
CAN-SPAM and PECR, and a mailbox provider's own promotional classification, all turn on
whether a message is functional. Genuinely promotional content can pull in an unsubscribe
requirement, and an unsubscribe link would be a second link, which this section's "one
action link each" forbids — so a line that reads as marketing costs the template the very
thing the checklist above buys it. A line describing what Dana is, sent to somebody who is
not yet a user, stays on the functional side of that boundary, because it tells the reader
what they are being invited to. The same line sent to an existing connection is filler,
and filler is what makes a functional message look like a marketing one. For the same
reason the product is named in a subject only as the place an event happened, and is never
described there.

**The same argument is written beside the templates**, at `ORIENTATION_FOOTER` in
`app/src/server/notify/templates.ts`, in the same CAN-SPAM and PECR terms and with the
discriminator stated the other way round — whether the recipient already knows what Dana
is. That is deliberate duplication and it is named here so it does not become accidental:
this section is where the "one action link each" rule the argument defends lives, and the
reader of that rule has no reason to grep a template module, while the reader editing a
template has no reason to open section 10. **Whichever of the two is edited, the other is
the copy to check**, and `dueLine` in the same module carries the second half of this
reasoning — why a due renders as the stored floating date and never as "Today".
