### 10.7 Email deliverability

One transactional provider serves invite, share-grant, and reminder templates. Launch
checklist: sending domain with SPF, DKIM, DMARC configured; from-address noreply@ the
product domain; short templates with one action link each. This is a named operational
setup cost, done once.

**Templates are HTML with a required text/plain alternative part.** A send carrying HTML
alone does not meet this section, and the text part says the same thing and carries the same
one action link rather than being a stub. "Short" and "one action link each" are what do the
deliverability work; inbox placement turns on the authentication above, sender reputation
and the text-to-link ratio, and well-formed multipart is what every transactional provider
sends.

**The orientation footer goes on the two invitations and on neither of the other two**, and
that boundary is a deliverability rule rather than taste. The transactional exemptions in
CAN-SPAM and PECR, and a mailbox provider's promotional classification, turn on whether a
message is functional. Promotional content can pull in an unsubscribe requirement, and an
unsubscribe link would be a second link, which "one action link each" forbids. A line
describing what Dana is, sent to someone not yet a user, stays functional because it tells
them what they are being invited to; the same line sent to an existing connection is filler,
and filler is what makes a functional message look like marketing. For the same reason the
product is named in a subject only as the place an event happened, never described there.

The same argument sits beside `ORIENTATION_FOOTER` in `app/src/server/notify/templates.ts`,
stated from the other side (whether the recipient already knows what Dana is). Whichever of
the two is edited, the other is the copy to check.
