### 10.2 Authorization enforcement points

**There is no single authorization chokepoint.** What counts as authorized depends on what
is being asked for, so no one middleware can decide it. Authorization is two mechanisms,
and anyone adding a route needs both.

**The structural half.** Every data query joins folder_members on the viewer *inside the
FROM clause*, so a query that forgets the join returns nothing rather than another person's
rows. Personal Labels apply the same shape to a second kind of scope: every statement
touching `task_personal_labels` or `folder_personal_labels` carries
`owner_user_id = <viewer>` where the membership join would sit, so a statement that forgets
it likewise returns nothing.

**The hand-written half.** Every mutation resolves the 5.4 matrix server-side before
touching rows, through `accessOf(...)` gates (`app/src/server/domain/folders.ts`) written by
hand in each domain module and route. **Adding a route means adding its gate. Nothing adds
it for you.** An id arriving in a query string or body (`GET /events?folder=`, `task_id`
on `PATCH /discussions/:id`, `folder_id` on `GET /api/v1/labels`, every `file_id` in a request body) is
gated by hand in the handler that reads it.

Two sweeps walk the mounted router and catch the commonest omissions; neither replaces the
gate.

- `app/tests/criteria/ac-236-structural-guard-sweep.test.ts` checks **authentication**:
  every POST, PATCH, PUT or DELETE carries a session and CSRF check, and every GET answers
  401 without a session, except the named public routes (the health probe, the two legs of
  OAuth sign-in, and the CSP report endpoint of 10.8), each entry carrying its reason. The
  list is asserted in both directions, so a stale or missing entry fails.
- `app/tests/criteria/ac-397-authorization-sweep.test.ts` checks **authorization** for every
  route whose *path* carries an id: it drives each as a signed-in stranger with no
  membership, authorship or connection to the row, expecting 404, or 403 where 5.4 or 7.15
  grants visibility and withholds the act. Its table of expected statuses is hand-written
  and asserted against the enumeration in both directions, so a new id-bearing route with no
  entry fails. Routes whose path parameter names no row of Dana's (4.20's place surfaces, the
  Openverse thumbnail proxy) carry an entry saying so.

What the sweeps do not see: ids outside the path, routers mounted more than one level deep,
and what a *member below the bar* is refused (the sweep's member holds Can edit). Those are
each asserted by their own criteria, or by a reviewer reading the gate.

**Status codes are three different facts and must not be swapped.**

- **404**: you cannot see this. Inaccessible resources answer 404, never 403, so a refusal
  never confirms an existence.
- **403**: you can see this and may not do this. Permitted-visibility but forbidden-action
  answers 403; it is legitimate only because the caller has already been shown the thing, so
  the refusal discloses nothing new. The Label routes answer 403 to a Folder member below the
  bar the act sits at (`label.vocabulary.manage` at Can edit; `label.apply` at Can edit, or at
  Can contribute over a Task assigned to them), with the resolver's reason as the message.
  Likewise `DELETE /api/v1/files/:id` answers 403, not 404, to anyone who can read the same
  bytes, the avatar serving predicate making that every signed-in account for an avatar.
- **422**: you can see this and it does not belong here, such as a Folder Label aimed at
  another Folder's task. Answering 422 where 404 is due would confirm an existence.

No endpoint trusts a folder_id, task_id, or user_id from the client without these checks.
The composite FK of 4.5 and the constraints of section 4 are the last line, not the first.
