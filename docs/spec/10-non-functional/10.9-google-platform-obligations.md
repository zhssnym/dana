### 10.9 Google platform obligations

Dana signs people in with Google and nothing else (10.1, 11.6), so Google's developer
terms bind the product. This section records what they require and how Dana meets it.

Three documents govern, and only three:

- the [Google API Services User Data
  Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy), which covers
  Google Sign-In by name;
- the [Google APIs Terms of Service](https://developers.google.com/terms/);
- the OAuth app verification requirements, in the [OAuth App Verification Help
  Center](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463073) and its [verification
  requirements](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321) page.

**Account deletion is not one of Google's obligations on Dana.** The User Data Policy and
the verification requirements state no account-deletion or data-export requirement. The
deletion rule is **Google Play's**: the Play Console's [account deletion
requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)
oblige an app offering in-app account creation to offer in-app deletion and a **web link**
at which deletion can be requested without reinstalling. Dana is a PWA on no store, so that
does not apply, and Dana implements deletion anyway (8.10, behind 9.7's typed confirmation,
described on the Privacy page). **If Dana is ever wrapped and listed on Play or the App
Store, the web deletion link becomes work.**

**What the User Data Policy requires.** A published privacy policy that fully documents how
the application accesses, uses, stores and shares Google user data, with its URL listed in
the OAuth client configuration; only the minimum scopes the product needs; no deceptive or
undisclosed use; and user data secure in transit and at rest. Dana's Privacy page names the
three Google-derived fields, what they are used for, and who they are shown to (9.2), and
10.1 fixes the scopes at `openid email profile`.

**Brand verification.** The verification requirements name four clauses: a homepage on a
domain the developer has verified, describing the product rather than being only a login
page; a privacy policy hosted on that same domain, linked from the homepage and from the
OAuth consent screen and **identical in both places**; verified ownership of every
authorized domain, through Search Console; and Google's sign-in branding on the button that
starts the flow. Dana meets the first two by construction, one origin serving `/`,
`/privacy` and `/terms` (9.2, 11.1). The other two are facts about the Cloud project rather
than this repository, and **must be confirmed by whoever administers it**: that the
authorized domain is verified, and that the consent screen's privacy URL is the same URL the
landing footer links.

**Scope class decides everything else, and Dana is in the mildest class.** `openid`, `email`
and `profile` are non-sensitive, so the Limited Use restrictions, the annual security
assessment and the annual re-verification attached to Sensitive and Restricted scopes do not
reach Dana. Verification is correspondingly not mandatory, with two consequences for opening
Dana up. An unverified app cannot show its name and logo on the consent screen, which needs
the lighter brand verification submission. And Google's text caps an unverified app at
**100 users**; whether that cap applies to a non-sensitive-scope app already in production is
ambiguous in Google's wording. The safe reading is that verification gates growth past 100
accounts, so brand verification is submitted before Dana is opened up rather than discovered
by hitting the cap. Requesting any scope beyond the three above changes this paragraph
entirely and is a decision, not an implementation detail.

**Data portability is the one obligation in the APIs Terms of Service that binds Dana.**
Section 3(d): "For as long as you use or store any user data that you obtained through the
APIs, you agree to enable your users to export their equivalent data to other services or
applications of their choice in a way that's substantially as fast and easy as exporting
such data from Google products and services, subject to applicable laws". Dana obtains a
name, an email address and a picture through Google Sign-In and stores them (4.1).
"Equivalent data" is ambiguous; Dana takes **the wide reading**, everything it holds about
the person, because the clause measures Dana against how easily Google hands the same data
over, and Google's answer is Takeout: a file, not a screen. [Section
7.22](../07-api/07.22-data-export.md) is how the obligation is met: a session-gated
download of one self-describing JSON document. A documented, versioned JSON file satisfies
"to other services or applications of their choice" as well as Takeout's archive does; a
specific interchange format, if ever asked for, is a new decision.
