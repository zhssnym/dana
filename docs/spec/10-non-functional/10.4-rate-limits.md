### 10.4 Rate limits

Per user: invites 5 per minute and 20 per day; invite resend 1 per invite per 24 hours;
search 30 requests per minute; general mutations 120 per minute; all others unlimited in
v1. Responses use 429 plus Retry-After. Enforcement is per deployment instance in v1
(single-instance hosting per section 11); revisit if horizontally scaled.

Added 2026-09-04 (issue #99): **CSP violation reports, 30 per minute, keyed on the
caller's address and not on a user.** It is the one row here that cannot be per user,
because the caller is a browser posting a report of its own accord (10.8) and has no
session to key on. Thirty is chosen from both ends: a real breakage on a cold load
produces one report per refused resource and the shell loads a handful, so thirty leaves
a wide margin above the case the endpoint exists to catch; and thirty bounds what a
stranger can spend of the free-tier Sentry quota 11.12 sizes for ordinary error volume,
which is the case it must not become. **The honest limit on "per address": this process
does not set Express's `trust proxy`, so behind the edge of 11.11 the address it reads is
the edge's and the bucket is per deployment rather than per visitor.** That is the
conservative direction — it bounds noise harder, never less — and thirty a minute is
still far above a real breakage; but nothing in this row should be read as identifying a
visitor, and changing `trust proxy` would move every limiter above at once and is
therefore not a change to make inside this one. **The consequence stated plainly, because
"conservative" hides it: behind the edge, thirty reports a minute from anywhere hold the
endpoint at 429 for everyone**, so one noisy browser can silence reporting for the whole
deployment for the rest of that minute. The log line and the budget of 10.8 make that
survivable rather than good, and every per-address limit in this section has the same
property today.
