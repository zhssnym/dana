### 10.4 Rate limits

The limited surfaces and their numbers are `RATE_LIMIT_POLICY` in
`app/src/server/security/rate-limit.ts`:

- **Per user**, keyed on the session: invites (per minute and per day), search, general
  mutations, and data export (7.22).
- **Per client address**, because the caller has no session to key on: CSP violation reports
  (10.8), and the two sign-in doors, `GET /api/v1/auth/google` and
  `GET /api/v1/auth/google/callback`, each with its own counter.
- **Invite resend** is a state guard rather than a rate limit: one resend per invite per
  window, anchored on `last_sent_at` (`RESEND_WINDOW_HOURS` in
  `app/src/server/domain/invites.ts`).

Everything else is unlimited. Every limit is refused the same way: 429 with `Retry-After`
and the 7.0 envelope. Enforcement is in-memory and per instance, which assumes the single
instance of section 11; a second instance needs a shared counter store.

**Why each unusual row exists.** The export is the only limit on a read, and its ground is
cost rather than abuse: it walks every table for one person and builds the whole document
in memory. The sign-in rows are also cost: a begin issues state, nonce and PKCE and writes a
cookie, and a callback is the only route that spends an outbound request to a third party
(Google's token exchange) on an unauthenticated caller's say-so. The CSP row bounds what a
stranger can spend of the Sentry quota 11.12 sizes for ordinary errors, while leaving a wide
margin above one real breakage's handful of reports. **The two sign-in counters are separate**
so that a stranger hammering one door cannot lock a real visitor out of the other.

**Per client means per client, not per deployment.** `createApp`
(`app/src/server/index.ts`) sets Express's `trust proxy` to the number of proxies in front of
the process, one: Render's edge, which terminates TLS. So the address a limiter keys on is the one the edge appended to
`X-Forwarded-For` rather than the edge's own. Two rules follow. **The number is never
`true`**: that trusts the whole header, which the client writes, and a caller presenting a
fresh address per request would meet no limit. And **nothing may be put in front of the edge
without moving the number**, since the count is a claim about the deployment; a CDN ahead of
the host makes it one more. This assumes the edge appends the caller to the header; 11.11
owns that assumption and the production check that settles it. If the edge appended nothing,
a caller-written header would go unchecked and the address-keyed limits would bind nobody.

**An address is not a person.** Many sign-ins from one NAT pool, office or carrier gateway
within a minute refuse the next, and that person sees a sign-in that will not start for a
reason nothing on the page explains. The trade is accepted at Dana's current scale, where the
alternative is no limit on the one route that spends an outbound request for an
unauthenticated caller. The first report of it raises the number, which was chosen with
margin above honest use rather than against a measurement of shared addresses.
