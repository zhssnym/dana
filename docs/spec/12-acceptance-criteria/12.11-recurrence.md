### 12.11 Recurrence

The rule subset, successor generation on completion and abandon, and calendar projection.

- AC-071 Given a rule outside the 4.11 subset (COUNT, or BYMONTHDAY 29 to 31), then 422.
- AC-072 Given weekly Monday due 2026-07-20 completed that day, then the successor is due 2026-07-27, carries the rule, and the completed row keeps its date with rule null.
- AC-073 Given the same task completed 2026-07-28, then the successor is due 2026-08-03 and no occurrence exists for 2026-07-27.
- AC-074 Given daily due 2026-07-20 completed 2026-07-24, then the successor is due 2026-07-25.
- AC-075 Given weekly Monday due 2026-07-27 completed 2026-07-25, then the successor is due 2026-08-03.
- AC-076 Given abandon on a recurring occurrence, then a successor generates exactly as on completion.
- AC-077 Given two concurrent completes on one occurrence, then exactly one successor exists and one completed_by is recorded.
- AC-078 Given UNTIL equal to the current due date, when completed, then no successor is created and the rule remains on the closing row.
- AC-079 Given uncomplete while the successor is pending and edited, then the successor is deleted, the rule returns, and the restored row is the current occurrence.
- AC-080 Given uncomplete while the successor is already completed, then 409.
- AC-081 Given steps on an occurrence, when completed, then the successor carries the same steps unchecked and no My Day entries.
- AC-082 Given a July 2026 month view with completed occurrences on the 13th and 20th and the current occurrence due the 27th, then each of those dates draws exactly one row, ghosts appear only after the 27th, and the 6th shows nothing.

**AC-072 to AC-075 are asserted by their content rather than at the dates they name**, the
same reading AC-046 and AC-050 carry in 12.7 and for the same reason: the completing side of
each worked example is today wherever the suite runs, the literal July 2026 dates are
producible by no API call, and owning the clock would decide for an implementer who has not
yet written a line that today comes from the process clock rather than the database, which 8.1
admits either way. Each criterion asserts the relationship its example exists to pin, derived
from 8.5's cutoff over today and a weekday-matched rule on any date: on time generating the
next slot, late skipping the passed slots so the calendar keeps an honest gap, early keeping
the alignment, and daily late without pile-up.

**AC-082 is deliberately outside that reading**: its July 2026 occurrences are history seeded
directly, and projection reads stored dates against the requested range, evaluating no today,
so it runs literally as written. Two fixture facts are recorded with it. Within a range of
exactly 2026-07-01 to 2026-07-31 the series projects nothing, the first instance after the
27th being 2026-08-03, so the ghost clause bites only on the padded month-grid range a month
view actually requests, still inside the 62-day cap. And Q3C scopes projection by accessible,
pending, and rule-carrying, never by the task's own due_date lying inside the range, so the
embedding query fetches what it projects from rather than projecting off the BETWEEN-bounded
real rows.

- AC-098 Given a calendar ghost clicked, then the current occurrence opens with the upcoming-occurrence banner.
- AC-142 Given two identical overdue recurring occurrences completed at one instant with tz values a calendar day apart, then their successors are dated exactly a day apart; given a present tz that is not an IANA identifier on complete or abandon, then 422 naming tz whatever the row holds; given an absent tz where the transition would run generation, then 422 naming tz with the row unchanged and no successor, and the same call with a zone transitions.
- AC-143 Given an accessible pending rule-carrying task whose current occurrence is due before the requested calendar range, then its instances inside the range are projected even though the task itself draws no real row there; given one whose current occurrence is due after the range, then nothing is projected.

**Why AC-143 is a criterion of its own, and why its two halves are both load-bearing.**
AC-082 cannot catch the violation, which is why this is a number rather than a clause inside
it: AC-082's own current occurrence is due the 27th and its grid opens on 2026-06-28, so its
series sits inside its own range and both implementations draw it identically. The wrong
implementation is also the shorter one, the endpoint already fetching real rows with due_date
BETWEEN start AND end, and projecting from that result set being less code than fetching what
it projects from separately. The failure is silent, nothing erroring and no row being wrong
while the array is merely shorter, and it empties precisely the month-ahead view where a
repeating task is most worth seeing. The criterion's second half is not redundant with its
first either: 8.5 bounds projection at both ends, lo = max(range_start, due_date plus one day)
and no instance later than range_end, so an implementation that dropped the range bound to
satisfy the first half would paint ghosts for a series whose current occurrence falls after
the range entirely.
