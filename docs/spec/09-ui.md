## 9. UI specification

Structure, states, and behavior only; no colors, typography, or spacing. Every page has five possible states unless narrowed: loading (skeleton rows), populated, empty (copy below), error (copy below), plus the global offline banner. Exact copy is normative; design may restyle, not reword, without a spec change.

### 9.1 Global chrome

- Sidebar: a navigation landmark and the application's only side chrome. Header carrying the app name (links to /app), the notifications bell (9.12), and the global search control (Ctrl+K or /). Then the page block, in fixed order: Desk, Today, Scheduled, Assignments, Important, All, plus any unpinned view the pin list of 9.7 has pinned (Done, Abandoned, Anytime, and the viewer's saved views), each present only when pinned and each a link; then a "More" row opening onto whatever native pages the pin list has not pinned, each a link, its open state the browser's (amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30: Done, Anytime and Abandoned ship unpinned and the search overlay was their only door). Then the Library block; then the footer.
- Library: a collapsible block headed "Library", holding the viewer's shelves and folders as one ordered list. A shelf is a collapsible group of folder rows headed by its name; folders on no shelf follow. Each folder row carries the folder's mark (its icon in its colour, 4.3), its name, and its pending count. The order is the viewer's own, seeded alphabetically and rearranged by dragging: rows reorder, move onto and off shelves, and two folders held together create a shelf; shelf headings drag as rows too. A group being closed is never sprung open by a drag hovering it; the drop reads "Into {shelf}".
- Footer: one primary "New folder" action, which opens the folder it makes; then a row of two acts of making — "New shelf" and "Import" (a workflow file) — each keeping its label at every width and both named by tooltip in every state. Then Trash, People and Settings as links; then the avatar menu ("Account": Help, Sign out). (Amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30, sidebar F3, F8 and F10: Trash is a destination and stands with the places, and Settings is offered once, as the link.) Below 768px the sidebar is a drawer, opened by a "Menu" control on a phone bar above the tab strip that also carries the mark, "Search" and the bell; the drawer closes on navigation, on its backdrop and on Escape. Below that width the task surface of 9.3 takes the whole width in either mode and draws no resize handle, there being nothing for a hand to drag against. The Library is the only place a shelf or folder is made from besides the shelf's own "+" and the shelf page; the word for a group of folders is "shelf" in every dialog, menu, toast and confirm, and a shelf may be made empty.
- Tab strip: a row of tabs above the window bar, session-scoped working state, never stored server-side. Every tab is the same tab: one anatomy (mark, title, close control), equal treatment, closable, and reorderable by drag. The place the viewer stands on is the active tab. "Open in a new tab" on a folder, shelf, or task appends a tab and goes to it; ordinary navigation retargets the active tab, as an address bar does. Closing the active tab lands on the neighbouring place; closing the last tab lands on Desk. A "+" beside the last tab opens any shelf or folder as a tab, or begins New folder, New shelf, New task, or Import. Every tab is the same width within one floor and one ceiling, and the tabs sit flush against one another rather than as separated pills: one to three are wide and even, a filling strip narrows them toward the floor rather than pushing the last one out, and past the floor the row scrolls. (Amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30, tabstrip F2, F3, F4, F9, F10 and F11: navigation to a place a tab already holds makes that tab active rather than retargeting another, and the "+" goes to that tab rather than opening a second; the active tab is drawn lifted against a tinted strip, the strip being ground and the tab a card; a focused tab closes on Delete or Backspace and any tab on a middle click, Ctrl+W being the browser's; and the sole remaining tab draws no close control, closing it having only ever meant going to Desk. Amended again at that round, tabstrip F13: the floor, the ceiling and the flush row are `prototype-v2`'s own, which the port had dropped for tabs sized to their own words with a gutter between them, and sharing the width between the two is this round's, the prototype drawing every tab at its floor however much room the strip had; and "New task" joins the acts the "+" begins, opening a plain task rather than one of 9.3's entry points, the strip belonging to the window and not to the page under it. New folder and New shelf are named there exactly as the footer names them, without an ellipsis, on this section's own rule for what an ellipsis means.)
- Window bar: a thin strip under the tabs belonging to the window, not the page. Its left end holds the active filter's removable chips; its right end holds the active tab's controls — the view strip, the filter control, the Activity line ("{verb} {ago}", opening the recent events of the thing shown), and the object's overflow menu. The page's title is not repeated in it; the tab already says it.
- The view strip: one control per place-page offering the views the page can honestly draw — list always; board wherever the page has a grouping (a board column captures with its own value, and dropping a card in a column writes that value); calendar only where having a date is what puts a task on the page, which is Scheduled. The chosen view is one client-side preference across pages; a page not offering the remembered view falls back to list without forgetting it. `?mode=` names a view in the URL. The strip is icons rather than written labels, and it rests open: every view the page offers is drawn at once, one segmented control (amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30; it rested collapsed until then, and a hover was the only way to learn that a page had a board or a calendar at all, which a touchscreen cannot make). The options it does not draw stay present and reachable, at no width rather than removed, so the keyboard and a screen reader always meet the whole group; only the current option holds the tab stop.
- The filter: one control with the same meaning on every place-page — due presence and ranges, assignee (multi-value, "Unassigned" included), important, repeating, created-by, show-abandoned, group-by, sort, and "Save as view". Active state renders as removable chips in the window bar, never as a count alone. A filter belongs to the page and to the session: kept on return to that page, never carried to another. Grouping replaces the page's own arrangement rather than nesting inside it.
- Capture: each page has one always-visible capture point. Quick-add rows sit where a page has a natural landing place (a section, a group, a list foot); in board view every column captures and the floating button is absent; the floating "Add task" button appears only on the pages with no capture affordance of their own — Today, Important, and Assignments — and never on Done or Abandoned. Today carries the quick-add row as well, at its list foot, since the polish round of 2026-08-30: every other list page has one and Today asking for its own separate word for the same job was the round's taskrow F12; the floating button stays because it is where the full form is reached from a page whose rows live elsewhere. Every capture creates the task with the entry defaults of 9.3 for where it stands, so a task made on a page appears on that page; where the list it was typed into carries a hand order (9.6), the new task lands at its foot, in typing order (polish round, 2026-08-30).
- Undo: one bar for the whole window, bottom-left, naming the act it can take back ("Deleted "{title}""), for five seconds, with Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z as its shortcut; no redo. Acts register their own inverse. The bar's sentence and 4.13's event are separately specified: the event is written server-side inside the mutation's transaction and the bar's wording is this section's, so neither is the source of the other (RFC 0005 change control item 57, B6, confirming item 44). Undo issues the inverse mutation — no write is ever held back waiting for the window to close.
- The mark: Dana's mark is drawn inline as SVG rather than fetched as an image, everywhere it appears (the sidebar header, the tab strip, the landing and invite pages, the identity page). Its rest state is the whole logo, so a mark that never animates is simply the logo; on hover of its enclosing link, button or menu item a pen mask uncovers it along the ribbon once and finishes rather than snapping back; each instance's mask id is unique; `prefers-reduced-motion` turns the motion off. The icon family is likewise animated: a glyph draws, wiggles or pops on the hover of its enclosing control, and a region may switch the motion off wholesale (the sidebar does, for its rows). (Folded from `prototype-v2` at RFC 0005 change control item 57, D: ledger L-117, L-141 to L-145.)
- Object acts: every kind of thing (task, folder, shelf, the surface) declares its acts once and every overflow menu draws them through one component, so where a person stands never decides what they may do to a thing. The acts sit in five groups in a fixed order — reach it, change it, copy it, stop it, destroy it — the destructive group last and behind a separator; stopping (Abandon, "I am not doing this") and destroying (Delete, "this should not exist") are two groups and never one. An act nothing supplies a handler for is not drawn, so a surface cannot offer what it cannot do; a surface may add rendered extra items of its own above the contract's acts and only rendered ones. A task's open act carries the full-screen glyph. (Item 57, D: L-002 to L-006, L-008, L-014.)
- The view strip's disabled option, where a page has one, names why in its tooltip instead of its name. The window bar is filled by a page portalling into it, so a page keeps its own state while drawing into shell chrome. A side panel takes room from the page rather than covering it; its width is one number in a store the shell reads, shared by every panel, so two cannot overlap. Menus, lists and pickers share one row primitive. (Item 57, D: L-034, L-136, L-138, L-139, L-214.)
- Library drag: the sidebar's sorting is pointer-driven rather than HTML5 drag-and-drop, so it works on touch and auto-scrolls; the carried row follows the pointer and the rows it passes slide out of its way by its own height, nothing being outlined; the same list sorts by keyboard, the handle a real button, arrows moving the item and focus following. The proposal pill of a hold rides the carried row rather than the row beneath, and the hold is drawn as a closing ring that starts from empty every time. (Item 57, D: L-090, L-091, L-093, L-127, L-128.)
- Offline banner (global, persistent while offline): "You're offline. Dana needs a connection; changes can't be saved right now." Writes attempted offline fail with the toast "Couldn't save. You're offline."
- Global error toast for failed requests: "Something went wrong. Nothing was saved. Try again." 409 conflicts show the state machine copy of 5.1 verbatim; a stale page-block write (4.17) uses the same treatment.

### 9.2 Landing and login

Public page: one page describing the shipping product, collaborative to-do lists in shareable folders; a single "Continue with Google" control; privacy and terms links. The page shows the product as the product renders it rather than as illustration. After callback, first-run lands on Desk at /app per 9.1's route table; pending invites, when any exist, gate the application first as 9.8 describes.

**The landing page describes what Dana does, never what it might do.** Every capability the page names must be one a person can use on the day they read it, and no copy may promise, imply or preview a capability Dana does not ship. This is stated as a rule because it is broken by addition rather than by omission: no assertion about what the page does contain can catch a sentence that should not be there, and the pressure to write one is highest exactly when the product is smallest. Its named instance, and the one AC-159 asserts, is that no copy anywhere on this page claims AI, which section 1 says v2 does not have and will not.

Copy and composition are the design record's, adopted by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-24, and are not transcribed here. This section fixes the rules copy is written under rather than the sentences it is written as, and that is the change the ruling made. A landing page is marketing, and marketing moves; a specification carrying its sentences would hold a second copy of the design record and drift from it on the first edit, silently, because no criterion can compare two passages of prose and say which one is stale. What a criterion can hold is a rule, so the rules are what this section keeps:

- No capability claimed that Dana does not ship, AI named as the instance above (AC-159).
- Exactly one "Continue with Google" control on the page. The same call to action in a hero and again in a closing block is two, and AC-159 counts the page rather than the hero (AC-159).
- Privacy and terms links (AC-159).
- The page names folders and says the product is shared (AC-159).

One string stays normative here, because it states a fact about what Dana takes rather than a claim about what Dana does, and its accuracy is a privacy obligation rather than an editorial choice. Under the sign-in control: "Dana requests only your Google profile and email for sign-in, sharing, and invitations."

The sign-in control is a link and not a button, which is behaviour rather than presentation and is stated because the design renders a button. It carries the viewer's detected timezone to the authorisation endpoint of section 7, which is how a first session gets a timezone before any row exists to hold one; a button with a click handler could carry it too, and a link is what makes the destination visible, bookmarkable and reachable with scripting unavailable. AC-159 admits either role deliberately and does not settle this.

Where the page animates a heading, the accessible name is the settled sentence and the animation is decorative. The adopted landing rotates one verb inside its headline and renders a single fixed sentence to assistive technology, which is what 9.1's addressing sentence requires of any name a person is read: a heading whose text depends on when it is sampled has no name at all.

The copy that shipped at P8 was the human owner's own, written on 2026-08-11 and recorded as closing section 13 item 14; the designer's replaced it on the owner's ruling of 2026-08-13. Both were tables of sentences in this section. Item 14 closes a third time on 2026-08-24 against the form above rather than against any wording, which is what that item was always protecting: it recorded the no-AI rule and not who held the pen, and that rule now has a section stating it and a criterion asserting it, neither of which depends on the copy staying still.

Unverified-email rejection page (10.1; AC-008): rendered by the callback in place of establishing a session when Google reports the email unverified; no rows are created. Copy, normative like all copy in this section: heading "We can't sign you in yet"; body "Your Google email address isn't verified. Dana uses your email for sharing and invites, so it needs Google to confirm it first. Verify your email with Google, then try again."; one action, a link labeled "Back to Dana", returning to the landing page.

### 9.3 The task (create and edit are one artifact, and the artifact is the task)

There is no task form. A task is its row, its card, and its page: the row expands in place into the card; the card is the same surface a dialog, a side panel, or a tab presents; and /app/tasks/:id is that surface as a page. Every edit commits as it is made — there is no Save control and no draft state — and the undo bar of 9.1 covers the destructive ones. Where a task opens (dialog, panel, or tab) is a client-side preference; a row offers the choice and remembers it, and "tab" opens the task at /app/tasks/:id, which is the card as a page (polish round, 2026-08-30: the setting accepted "tab" and opened the dialog regardless, over a read-only page whose one button popped that same dialog; two of the round's fixers reached this sentence, one proposing that the third option go and one building what it promised, and the built one is what shipped, so 9.7's Appearance control still names three). The side panel makes the page narrower rather than covering it. A task opened as a tab carries its own window-bar controls, and its folder and section stand at the bar's left.

The card holds, in order: the title (editable in place, required); a header strip of chips — due date, folder, assignee, reminder, repeat — each opening its picker in place; then Steps, Notes, and Attachments as blocks; then Comments (9.6's conversation rules apply); then a footer toolbar carrying the completion control, the star, and "More actions" (History, Copy link, Duplicate, Move to folder, Make this a folder, Abandon or Restore, Delete). "Delete task…" confirms with "Move this task to Trash? It stays there for 30 days." (the selection bar counts its plural), and the footer renders the task's creation and its completion; the rest of its history is the Activity control (polish round, 2026-08-30). The pickers open on the answer, not the instrument: resolved presets first, a calendar or list behind them, one shape at one width.

Row and card decisions carried from `prototype-v2` (RFC 0005 change control item 57, D). A row carries its section after its folder chip, read as a path ("Travel › Venue & vendors"), and omits it where the row already sits under that section's heading (L-153). Two densities, one component: two lines is the default because a truncated title does not announce itself where fewer rows do; the two shapes may differ in height and in where the details sit and in nothing else; a bare task costs one line (L-076, L-077, L-148, L-150). The details on a row are the controls that change them: the due and repeat chips open the same pickers the card opens (L-151). One model turns a task's fields into words for the row and the card alike, and a person's tint is deterministic from the model so one person is one colour on every surface (L-070, L-071). A row's own rename handler, where a surface supplies one, wins over the card's (L-074); on the shipped row the title is text and renaming is the card's, per item 47(e) (L-152 exempted). An empty block draws the control that fills it rather than nothing (L-157). The surface's own strip offers the tab only where the surface can hand the task to the page bar, a tab being an action rather than a state (L-037); Escape closes the surface and its chrome, and the card's footer offers no second close (L-163).

Moving keeps the unassign warning: "Moving will unassign Sara; she isn't a member of Home." Deleting moves the task to Trash with the undo bar, and no confirmation: two ways back is why the question left. The completion control and the history read stay: the footer renders the task's own events from 4.13 — created, completed, and the rest — actor and viewer-local time, "Deleted user" fallback unchanged.

### 9.4 Today

Structure: the shared page head (the page's mark, title, editable description, and the fact "N of M done"); then the Carried group (heading "Carried over"); the Today group (heading "Today"); and the recap collapsed at bottom (heading "Done today (N)").

The important-only narrowing is the window bar filter's important switch; the page's header carries no control of its own for it, because two controls for one variable forty pixels apart is how the two disagree. `?state=important` (and any link that means "Today, important only") seeds that filter rather than a page-local switch.

Row elements are the row's own (9.3), with this page's emphasis rules: a carried row keeps its "Carried over" words and drops the red — a warning true of every row in the group warns nobody; the pulled marker and its "Remove from My Day" un-pull control carry from v1 unchanged in behavior, the control reading "Remove from Today"; an unfiled task's folder chip says Desk. Abandon-from-here copy carries: "Abandon this task? It stops appearing in Today but stays everywhere else." Empty state per Q1. Error state: global toast plus inline retry.

A task row opens that task, on every page that draws one and whatever the task's status — the rule of v1's 9.4, unchanged, now through the surfaces of 9.3.

### 9.5 Scheduled, Assignments, Important, All, and the unpinned views

- Scheduled: everything with a date. The list is Q3's order under a ladder of headings — Today, Tomorrow, the days of this week, then week and month rungs — headings being structure, not controls; a row says "Due tomorrow" about one task and a heading says "Tomorrow" about a bucket, one date vocabulary at two altitudes. The calendar keeps Q3C whole: day, week, month zooms; previous/next/today; six-week month grid, Monday start; all-day before timed; times prefix timed chips at every zoom; ghost styling and the banner "Upcoming occurrence of a repeating task"; empty cell creates with that date; "+N more" is a control opening the day. An abandoned chip at month zoom quiets to its glyph and a faded tone, the strike appearing only at day and week. Mode and zoom persist client-side; `?mode=` reaches them by URL.
- Assignments: who is carrying what — every open task the viewer can read, grouped by person, the viewer's own group first, "Unassigned" last; the "from" half of a row names the creator, Dana recording no assigner. The board groups by person; dropping a card on a person assigns them. Capture assigns the viewer. The page's spine is a whose-assignments scope control in the window bar, resting on everyone: the sidebar entry's name never changes and the value it shows lives in that control, the rule every page follows (item 57, D: L-046, L-098).
- Important: single list per Q4; un-star removes immediately; the star's two names are 9.1's. On this one page the star rests unfilled, because a filled star on every row of the starred page distinguishes nothing, and its tooltip turns outward to name the task's home with the fact that home would show, its date for a dated row and its folder for an undated one, in 6 Q4's own words. Everywhere else the tooltip confirms importance.
- All: locks nothing — Q5's union rendered whole, with completed work carried in a collapsed section at the foot. Section labels are static.
- Done: unpinned by default (the pin list governs); day-grouped infinite scroll per Q6; rows drop the spent due-date echo under headings that already group by completion; "Mark as not done" with the G-SERIES successor confirmation verbatim; no capture anywhere on the page.
- Abandoned: an unpinned view of the one status with no other home; faded, struck, chip and "Restore" per the row's rules; no capture, because a task made pending would be filtered off the page it was made on.
- Anytime: an unpinned view of undated open work (Q2's rows, ungrouped).

Completing is immediate on the wire, as v1 ruled: no write is held back. What v2 adds is the undo bar of 9.1, which takes a completion back by issuing the inverse transition as a new mutation — the same "Mark as not done" a person could press on Done, offered for five seconds where they stand.

### 9.6 Folder page

The folder page is a page in the full sense: a cover it can be given; its mark (the identity control — pressing it opens the icon-and-colour picker, writing 4.3's shared identity); its name (inline rename, owner only; members read a heading); its editable description; and its body — the loose open tasks with a quick-add, then the page's blocks (sections, paragraphs, images, per 4.17) with tasks assigned to sections, a capture per section, an insert affordance between blocks, and the Done section collapsed at the foot (named "Completed" until the polish round of 2026-08-30: the finished pile was Done in two places and Completed in three, which reads as two states). Its board groups by section, and the column holding the tasks under none is named "No section" where the page has sections and "Tasks" where it has none (amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30: "No section" on a page with no sections read as something missing); while the board is shown the Done section is not drawn beneath it, the Done column being the one place a finished task stands. Ordering within sections and the block arrangement are the folder's, shared by every member; the filter of 9.1 narrows the page without touching its arrangement. The folder's conversation has two homes — a closed section at the page's foot, or the side panel — switched from the surface itself; a message is told apart by tone, never hue; likes are the whole reaction set.

Who may do what, the role matrix (RFC 0005, recording DES-225):

| Act | Owner | Member |
| - | - | - |
| Rename, Delete | yes | no — the name is the folder's address, and delete ends it for everybody |
| Leave | only via ownership transfer (below) | yes |
| Tasks: create, edit, complete, abandon, delete-to-Trash | yes | yes |
| The page: blocks, cover, icon, colour, description | yes | yes — the event log says who |
| History, Copy link, Duplicate, Save as workflow | yes | yes |
| Share, remove a member | per the 5.4 matrix, unchanged | per 5.4 |
| Shelf placement, sidebar arrangement, label colour | personal, always | personal, always |

A surface offers exactly the acts the viewer's role permits — Leave where Delete would be, never both — and the server refuses the rest regardless (10.2). Deleting moves the folder to Trash for every member at once, with the undo bar and no confirmation; members are notified (4.14). Leaving confirms — the way back is an invitation, not a Trash row — writes no Trash entry, offers no undo, and records "Left": "Leave Work? You lose access to it, and anything in it assigned to you becomes unassigned." An owner with other members leaves only by transferring ownership first (7.16), choosing a successor, defaulting to the longest-standing member per 8.9's order; a sole owner is offered Delete instead, 5.3's rule unchanged. Remove-a-member keeps its v1 confirmation verbatim. Each confirmation renders as one sentence, restyled never reworded.

Duplicate takes the structure and never the grants or the progress: tasks, sections, paragraphs, and section assignments copy; members, sharing, the conversation, assignees, and completion do not; spent due dates re-anchor on today and future ones keep, and the bar says which happened (L-055). The copy is the duplicator's own private folder, and its tasks are assigned to whoever made the copy, never left empty, so the copy appears in the duplicator's Assignments (item 44's B10, checked against `domain/acts.ts` at item 51(h) and pinned by AC-277; recorded here at item 57). The History sheet groups by day and names the actor on every line, and its lines and the row's popover are one sentence drawn through one formatter (item 57, D: L-199, L-200).

The sharing panel carries v1's spec unchanged: member list with joined dates and remove controls per 5.4, roster picker, email invite, pending list with Cancel and Resend ("Resent recently" inside 24 hours).

The "Label and color" dialog is retired: the folder's icon and colour are the shared identity above, set from the mark; a member's personal shelf placement is "Move to shelf" on the row; and 4.4's per-member color column is no longer written by any v2 surface (existing values are simply unused — the column is frozen and stays).

### 9.8 People and invites

People screen (reachable from the sidebar footer or Settings; nothing in the roster says human, an entry being an entry whatever it is, L-096): roster list with remove control ("Remove connection? You can still see shared folders; you'll just need a fresh invite to share new ones."); bare invite by email, refused when the address is already a connection ("You're already connected to this person. They're in your People list.", the 409 of 7.5 entered by owner ruling on 2026-08-09).
Invite gate: when Q12 is non-empty, the pending invites render in place of the application rather than inside it. It is one screen, headed "Invitations", carrying each invite as a row reading "Hassan invited you to Wedding" (or "Hassan invited you to connect") with Accept and Decline; the sidebar, the page beneath and the rest of the chrome are not rendered. Answering the last invite ends the gate, and accepting a folder invite lands inside that folder. The gate is the only surface that lists pending invites, and no page inside the application carries them.
Assignment chip: rows and the form show "Invite pending: sara@example.com" while an assignment-invite is outstanding.

Where that list lives was ruled at the second P8 change control on 2026-08-11 and is changed here by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-13, recording the designer's DES-081. The earlier ruling made it a section of My Day above everything else on that page, choosing that over two rejected readings: a band above every page, which would have been global chrome 9.1 did not name, and a surface appearing once after callback, which would have left anyone reloading before answering with no invite in front of them. The gate answers the second objection the same way, being a standing condition on the account rather than a moment after a callback, and it answers the first by not being chrome at all. What changed is the weighing of the first: this section says the invites render before anything else, and a section at the top of one page is before anything else only for a person who happens to be looking at that page, while a gate is what that phrase plainly says. The cost, stated because it is real, is that a person holding an unanswered invite cannot reach the rest of Dana without answering or declining it, which is the thing a gate is and the thing a section is not.

The gate is a region with no name, which is a decision and not an omission, and it is the earlier ruling's reasoning unchanged. 9.1's sections rule takes a region's name from the heading this section gives it or from the datum it is generated from, and this list has neither: it is one list rather than one per folder. Its rows are addressed the way a task row's controls are addressed, from the row's own sentence, which is what that sentence is for: "Hassan invited you to Wedding" tells a reader and a test which Accept belongs to which invite. AC-172 asserts the gate and is rewritten by RFC 0002, its present text asserting the section this ruling replaces.

The People screen carries no invite list, corrected at the P8R mid-package change control on 2026-08-13 on the Adversarial Test Engineer's escalation, which found that it could not be asserted because it could not be reached. When the gate arrived at acceptance, the sentence giving the People screen the same list was carried over from the ruling the gate replaced, and the two do not fit: the gate renders at every /app path while Q12 is non-empty, /app/people is an /app path, so the one page that was to hold the list is the one page unreachable exactly while the list would have anything in it. The sentence described a surface no person could ever see. Two repairs were available and the other is recorded so it is not reintroduced: exempting /app/people from the gate would have kept the list at the cost of a hole in a gate whose entire point is that invites come first, and it would have given a person two places to answer an invite where the ruling's argument was that they should have one that finds them. The list goes instead, which costs nothing, because what it was for was "where a person returns to an invite they have not answered" and the gate is now that place by construction: a person who reloads, or who navigates anywhere in the application, arrives at it.

### 9.9 Search overlay

Opened by the sidebar's search control or shortcut (the control is 9.1's "Search"; the overlay itself is a dialog named "Search Dana", the prototype's own, which 9.1's names table does not reach — item 57, A2); input with 300 ms debounce; before anything is typed it offers the pages of 9.1's block as rows to go to, the prototype's own reason being that they are few and fixed and cost one glance, where every folder and shelf the viewer has would be the sidebar drawn twice. Results group by kind: tasks (matching title, notes, and step titles), folders and shelves (name and description), and pages (matching a page's own prose — sections, paragraphs, captions). Task results keep the Pending, Abandoned, Completed order of Q8, each row wearing its status as a badge rather than standing under a status heading, because two classification schemes as peers is the fault DES-152 took out of the sidebar; within a kind a title match ranks above a body match. (Amended toward `prototype-v2` at RFC 0005 change control item 55, and at the polish round of 2026-08-30, search F5, F7 and F8: the pages are matched as they are typed as well as offered before anything is, so a place is findable by its name or by its question; Pending is announced rather than drawn, the badge being worn by Completed and Abandoned alone, a chip that says "ordinary" on nearly every row saying nothing; and the overlay has one close control, the "×", the Esc key having moved from a cap in the header that read as a button into the footer's hints beside the arrows.)

### 9.10 Shelves

A shelf is the folder page one level up: a per-user arrangement of folders with a page of its own — cover, mark (icon and colour), name (inline rename; renaming rewrites the member labels and re-addresses the page in one act), editable description, and blocks. Folder rows appear on the page as blocks among the writing: their presence is the membership's fact, their position the page's arrangement; a folder row under a heading folds with it; pressing one opens that folder as its own tab. An "Add folder" row offers every folder not on the shelf (never Desk) and "New folder…". The shelf's overflow carries its contract: History, Rename, Copy link, Duplicate (the page, no folders — a folder is on one shelf), Save as workflow, Delete. Deleting a shelf unlabels its folders and keeps them. A shelf offers no views and no filter: a view redraws tasks and a shelf holds folders; its list is its label, and narrowing it would be editing it. Shelves are private by construction — no link to send grants anything, no members exist (DES-195/196); the page's address is still copyable, an address not being an invitation.

### 9.11 Trash

One page listing every deleted task and folder the viewer can still reach, inside the 30-day window of 4.3/4.5, newest first, each row naming what it was, where it lived, who deleted it and when (from 4.13). Restore puts the thing back where it was as exactly one row; a restored folder returns with its membership intact. Anyone with access to the object may restore it — Trash is scoped by access, not by who deleted. The page states the rule once, under its heading: "Deleted items are kept for 30 days, then removed."; each row's own share of the window reads "30d left". Its tab is named "Trash" and wears the page's mark, and a row's Restore and delete are drawn at rest rather than on hover. (Amended at the polish round of 2026-08-30: the rule had two wordings, one for a full page and one for an empty one; the tab was a blank with a close cross; and a row whose only controls appear on hover is a row with nothing to press.) Empty state: "Nothing deleted", with the line "Tasks and folders you delete land here first, so a wrong press costs a click rather than the thing itself." No capture anywhere on the page. Each row whose folder the viewer owns also offers a permanent delete, "Delete for ever" behind a confirm that names the thing and says the act cannot be undone, and the page offers "Empty trash" over those rows while it holds any; a member who does not own the folder sees Restore alone (7.13 as amended by RFC 0005 change control items 36, 38 and 53; built at S28b, item 56). ### 9.13 First run and the starter workflows

A person signing in for the first time arrives at Desk (9.1) holding nothing. That empty Desk is the one screen every account sees and no account keeps, and it is where the product either teaches itself or does not.

Beneath Desk's own empty state, a fresh account is offered the starter workflows: bundled workflow files (7.16) that import with one press, each a worked example of a real piece of work rather than a tour of the interface. The purpose is navigation. A person who imports one has a folder in their Library, a page with sections, and tasks reaching the views of 9.4 and 9.5, which teaches where things live by putting something in each place rather than by describing it.

**Fresh** means the account holds no folder but Desk and no task anywhere. Both facts are already in what the client read to draw the page, so the offer needs no endpoint of its own. It stops being drawn the moment either is false, which includes the moment an import lands, and it does not return: a person who imports one or dismisses it has left first run, and asking again would be the product failing to notice it had already been answered.

**Each starter is an ordinary import.** POST /api/v1/workflows/import (7.16) takes the file, through the same endpoint and the same validation a person's own exported workflow goes through. Nothing is seeded, nothing is special-cased in the server, and no row is created that a person could not have created themselves. This is why the feature costs no backend, and it is also why it cannot drift from the import: a starter that stopped importing would fail the same way any workflow file would.

**What a starter may contain is what a workflow file may contain**: a folder name, task titles, and page blocks. It follows that a starter carries no comment and no reminder, and this section does not enforce that, because 7.16's schema has no key for either and a constraint the format makes impossible needs no rule. What is recorded here is why it is right that the format forbids them, so that a later widening of the format does not take these two with it by accident.

A comment would have to be attributed to somebody. Every comment in Dana is a thing a person said (4.15), and a fabricated one on a fresh account would be the product inventing a colleague, which is a lie about the one subject a collaboration tool cannot afford to lie about. A reminder would send email about a plan the person did not make, at an hour they did not choose, most probably after they had forgotten the starter was there; the person likeliest to receive it is the one who never came back, and a starter that mails a person who has left is the worst version of this feature.

**Every starter says in its own page that it can be deleted**, as the first paragraph block of its folder, because the offer's own copy is gone the moment it is answered and the page is what the person still has.

Copy, normative:

| Element | Copy |
| - | - |
| The offer's heading | "Start with an example" |
| The offer's body | "Import a worked example to see how Dana holds a real piece of work. Nothing here is fixed: change anything, or delete it all." |
| Dismiss | "No thanks" |
| The first paragraph block of every starter folder | "This is an example, and it is yours now. Change anything you like, or delete the whole folder from its menu when you are done with it." |

The starter files themselves are the design record's, on the 9.2 ruling of the same date: this section fixes that they exist, what they may hold, and what they must say, and does not transcribe them. What they must be is worked examples of problems a person actually has, each one solved the way Dana solves it, because a starter that demonstrates the interface rather than the work teaches a person how to click and not why to return.

**The sample workspace, and where the offer stands beside it (RFC 0005 change control item 60, 2026-08-30).** A brand-new account no longer arrives holding nothing: 7.1 writes the sample workspace of 7.20 in the provisioning transaction, so the first Desk a person sees is full, and the offer above is not drawn, its freshness test being false while the sample's folders stand. Above Desk and Today, while the sample is present, stands a strip: one sentence, "This is a sample workspace to show you around.", and two controls, "Remove sample data" and "Keep it". Remove confirms once, "Remove the sample workspace? Everything it added goes; anything you made yourself stays.", then removes through DELETE /sample and offers the undo bar reading "Sample workspace removed", whose inverse is POST /sample. A sample folder removal left standing because it is shared is said in a toast. Keep it dismisses the strip on this device only; 9.7's row is the way back. Removal empties the account, so the offer above is drawn again exactly when it should be: after the person has chosen to start from nothing.
