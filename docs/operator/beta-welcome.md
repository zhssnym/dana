Thanks for trying this.

Dana is a to-do app built around shared folders. You make a folder, put tasks in
it, and share the whole folder with someone — they see everything inside it, can
add to it, and can take things on. Whether that idea actually works for real
people doing real work is the thing we most need to find out, and it is the one
thing we cannot find out by ourselves.

## Getting in

1. Go to **https://dana.so**
2. Press **Continue with Google**.

That is the whole sign-up. Dana asks Google for your profile and your email
address and nothing else — it has no access to your Gmail, your Calendar, or
your contacts.

## Please install it on your phone

Dana runs in a browser but installs like an app, and it is a noticeably
different product once it is sitting on your home screen. It takes about a
minute and it is the step people most often skip.

**iPhone or iPad** — open https://dana.so in **Safari**. This will not work from
Chrome. Tap the Share button, scroll down the list, and tap **Add to Home
Screen**.

**Android** — open https://dana.so in Chrome, tap the **⋮** menu, and tap
**Install app** (on some versions it reads *Add to Home screen*).

**Mac or Windows** — in Chrome or Edge, look for a small install icon at the
right-hand end of the address bar and click it. If it isn't there, open the
**⋮** menu and look for **Install**.

## What is real here

**Your account is real, and everything you put in it stays.** This is not a
sandbox that gets wiped when the test ends. What you build is yours and carries
on afterwards. So please put actual work in — a list full of "test 1, test 2"
looks fine and teaches us nothing.

**The emails are real.** Dana sends four kinds: an invitation to a folder, a
request to connect, a notice that something has been shared with you, and task
reminders. If you invite someone, a genuine email arrives in their inbox with
your name on it. Invite people who are expecting to hear from you.

## What isn't there yet

So that you don't spend your time reporting things we already know:

- **No AI**, anywhere in the product. This version does not have it.
- **Reminders arrive as email**, not as phone notifications.
- **Dates are picked, not typed** — writing "tomorrow at 5" won't do anything.
- **No calendar connection**, Google or otherwise.
- **Dana needs a connection.** It does not work offline.
- **It is not in the App Store or Play Store**, and won't be during the test.

## What we're actually asking of you

Not a checklist of features to try. One thing:

> **Use Dana for one real piece of work, for one week, with at least one other
> person.**

A house move, a project at work, a trip, a course — anything with genuinely
moving parts and somebody else involved.

The most valuable single thing you can do is **share a folder with someone and
actually run something through it together.** If you use Dana alone you will
only meet half of it, and it is the less interesting half.

## Telling us what happened

Just reply to this email, or write to **<FEEDBACK ADDRESS>**. There is no form
and no bug tracker to learn.

A useful report is four lines:

- what you were trying to do
- what you expected
- what actually happened
- what you were using (iPhone, Android, laptop — and which browser)

A screenshot is worth more than a description of a screen.

**Please send the vague ones too.** *"I lost track of where my tasks had gone"*
is more useful to us than a tidy bug report, because that is exactly the kind of
thing we can no longer see for ourselves. Confusion, a rough edge, something you
went looking for and could not find, something you wanted that isn't there — all
of it counts, and none of it is too small.
